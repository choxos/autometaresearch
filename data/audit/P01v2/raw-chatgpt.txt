## 1. Why this design exists

R01 found that meta-research articles about data sharing point at a repository of their own
about four times as often as meta-research articles on other subjects. That result is
exploratory and cannot be made confirmatory after the fact, for three reasons stated on its
own page: the grading rule was developed by reading the analysis data, three of four
sensitivity analyses were post hoc, and no human validation was done.

This specifies a confirmatory test of the same hypothesis, on a sample R01 did not use,
with every instrument fixed before any data are retrieved.

## 2. Objective and hypothesis

**Primary objective.** To test whether a meta-research article whose subject is data sharing,
code availability or research transparency is more likely than a meta-research article on
another aspect of research practice to carry an actionable data availability statement of its
own.

**Primary hypothesis.** It is, and the association is directional.

**Primary null.** The odds ratio for the transparency exposure is 1.

**What would falsify the hypothesis.** An adjusted odds ratio whose 95 percent confidence
interval includes 1, in a sample powered as in section 7. Section 7 fixes the power in
advance so that a null can be read as evidence of absence rather than absence of evidence,
which was not true of R01.

**Prior expectation, stated so it cannot be revised.** R01 estimated 4.40 (1.80 to 10.75) on
a different sample with a rule developed on that sample. Regression to the mean and the
removal of rule-development bias both predict a smaller effect here. An estimate between 1.5
and 3 would be consistent with R01; an estimate at or above 4 would be surprising and should
itself be treated with suspicion rather than as confirmation.

## 3. Design

Cross-sectional study of published articles. The unit of analysis is one article.

## 4. Sample

### 4.1 Frame

The frame is the qualified tier B search described in [methods](../methods.qmd), which ANDs a
topic block with a study-of-literature qualifier. Measured in PubMed on 2026-09-16:

| block | qualified hits | already in tier A | available to this study |
|---|---|---|---|
| `datashare` | 754 | 27 | **727** |
| `report` | 2392 | 103 | **2289** |
| `reg` | 4450 | 88 | **4362** |
| `repro` | 229 | 14 | **215** |

**Every record already present in the tier A corpus is excluded**, by PMID, before screening.
That exclusion is what makes this sample independent of R01, and it is applied first so that
no later decision can reintroduce an overlapping record.

### 4.2 Eligibility

**Include** an article when all of the following hold:

1. Retrieved by the qualified tier B search for one of the four blocks above.
2. Not present in the tier A corpus harvested on 2026-09-16.
3. Screened as meta-research: its object of study is published research, researchers, journals
   or the research process, rather than patients, organisms, materials or clinical outcomes.
4. Present in PMC with a `<body>` element longer than 500 characters, so that a statement
   could have been extracted had one existed.

**Exclude** an article when any of the following hold:

1. Its publication type is comment, editorial, letter, news, biography or historical article.
   R01 estimated an odds ratio of 0.04 for this group; an article with no data of its own
   belongs to a different population and its absence of a statement is not evidence about
   sharing behavior. R01 adjusted for this. This protocol excludes it, which is cleaner.
2. It is a retracted publication or a retraction notice.
3. It is not in English. R01 applied no language limit; a statement in another language would
   be graded `none` by an English-language rule, which is a measurement failure rather than a
   finding.

### 4.3 Screening

Criterion 3 is the one no boolean query settles, and the reason tier B was never harvested for
the map. Screening is on title and abstract, by two models from different providers, each given
the same written criterion and blind to the other.

- Both agree include: included.
- Both agree exclude: excluded.
- Disagreement: adjudicated by the author. The count of disagreements is reported.

**Before screening the frame**, 200 records sampled at random across the four blocks are
screened by the author to create a reference standard. Agreement is reported as Cohen's kappa
per model. **If neither model reaches kappa 0.75 against the reference standard, screening by
model is abandoned** and the study proceeds on a random subsample screened entirely by the
author, with the sample size reduced accordingly and the power recomputed and reported.

## 5. Exposure

**Exposed**: retrieved by the qualified `datashare` block and screened in.

**Unexposed**: retrieved by the qualified `report`, `reg` or `repro` blocks, screened in, and
not also retrieved by the `datashare` block.

An article retrieved by `datashare` and by another block is **exposed**. The exposure is
whether transparency is among the article's subjects, not whether it is the only one.

This is a coarser exposure than a judgment of what an article is primarily about, and that is
deliberate: it is reproducible from a query string, and its misclassification is toward the
null. The alternative, asking a model what each article is mainly about, adds a second
instrument needing its own validation to measure something the query already approximates.

## 6. Outcome

### 6.1 Extraction

The data availability statement is taken from the JATS full text by
`studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/extract.py` **as it stands at commit
`158164be700a596610a074533e6509ce142a6774`**, unchanged. A prose pin is not a pin; that hash is the pin, and
`git show 158164be700a596610a074533e6509ce142a6774:studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/extract.py` is how a reader checks it. That code searches `<front>` and `<back>` only, never `<body>`, in
this order: a section or notes block with a `sec-type` or `notes-type` naming data
availability; a section or notes block whose title matches the data availability pattern; a
`custom-meta` pair whose name matches it.

The restriction to front and back matter is not a detail. Without it, a paper *about* data
availability has its own methods and results read as its disclosure, which is the artifact
that contaminated 4 of 43 exposed records in R01 and is documented there.

### 6.2 Grading

Three ordered levels, graded by the rule in `studies/_shared/R/grade.R` at that same commit, reproduced here
in full so that no later edit can pass unnoticed:

| grade | definition |
|---|---|
| `none` | no statement in front or back matter |
| `declarative` | a statement that shares nothing: available on request, contained within the article or its supplementary files, or not applicable |
| `actionable` | a named repository, or a DOI or URL that is not the article's own |

Tests apply in this order, and the order is itself pre-specified because changing it changes
the result:

1. A named repository present, from the fixed list in `REPO`: **actionable**. A repository
   outranks a co-occurring on-request clause, because an article that deposits its code and
   separately withholds licensed raw data has shared what it could.
2. An on-request clause present: **declarative**.
3. A not-applicable clause present: **declarative**.
4. A DOI or URL present that is not the article's own DOI: **actionable**.
5. Otherwise: **declarative**.

**Primary outcome**: `actionable` versus not.

**Secondary outcome**: the three-level ordinal grade.

### 6.3 Validation of the grading rule

**This is a gate, not a report.** 150 statements, stratified by rule grade, are graded by the
author, blind to the article's exposure and to the rule's grade. Agreement is reported as
Cohen's kappa with a 95 percent confidence interval.

- **Kappa at or above 0.80**: the rule is used as the primary instrument.
- **Kappa 0.60 to 0.79**: the rule is used, and the primary analysis is repeated on the
  hand-graded subsample alone as a co-primary, with both reported together regardless of
  whether they agree.
- **Kappa below 0.60**: the rule is abandoned. The study proceeds on a hand-graded random
  sample, with power recomputed and reported.

The rule is not modified after seeing the validation. If validation shows it is wrong, the
wrongness is reported and the fallback above is taken. This is the commitment R01 could not
make because its rule was written while the data were being read.

## 7. Sample size and power

**Computed by probes P1 to P5 on 2026-09-16. Every number in this section is emitted by
`studies/P01v2-confirmatory/out/probes.json` and is not retyped from anywhere.**

### Realized counts

| step | exposed | comparison |
|---|---|---|
| frame, after removing 205 tier A overlaps from 7617 records | 704 | 6526 |
| carrying a PMCID | 547 | 3770 |
| with PMC full text, the analysis set before screening | **520** | **3435** |

The exposed arm is 520, not the 260 the discarded assumption produced. The design
is better powered than it was written to be.

### The probe that changed the design

**PMC coverage differs sharply between arms: 77.7 percent of
exposed records against 57.8 percent of comparison records.**
The discarded assumption was a flat 60 percent for both, close to tier A's
59.5 percent, and it would have concealed a
20 point differential.

This is not a nuisance; it is adjacent to the hypothesis. Papers about data sharing are much
likelier to be openly available, which is itself a transparency behavior. The consequence for
this study is that the two arms are selected differently into the analysis set, and section 9
carries it as a disclosed threat that the design cannot remove.

### The baseline rate, and a reasoning error the probe caught

On the unscreened frame the comparison arm is 3.8 percent
actionable and the exposed arm 18.3 percent.

A comment in the probe code asserted that the unscreened comparison rate would be an **upper**
bound on the screened one, because primary studies carry journal-mandated statements. That was
backwards. R01's rate among screened meta-research was 20.5 percent against
3.8 percent here, so screening removes records that
deposit *less* often and **raises** the comparison rate. The unscreened figure is a lower bound.

The power calculation therefore runs across a range of plausible screened baselines rather than
at any single assumed one.

### Detectable odds ratio at 80 percent power, alpha 0.05

Rows are the screened comparison-arm actionable rate; columns are the proportion of records
surviving screening, which shrinks both arms.

| baseline rate | yield 0.5 | yield 0.7 | yield 1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.038 | 2.18 | 1.97 | 1.79 |
| 0.1 | 1.72 | 1.6 | 1.49 |
| 0.15 | 1.6 | 1.5 | 1.41 |
| 0.205 | 1.53 | 1.44 | 1.36 |
| 0.25 | 1.5 | 1.41 | 1.34 |

**Across the whole grid the detectable odds ratio is at most 2.18.**
R01's point estimate was 4.40 and section 2 states that anything between 1.5 and 3 would be
consistent with it. The design detects effects across that entire band, which is the property
R01 lacked: its floor was roughly 3.7, so it could only ever have seen an effect as large as
the one it reported.

A null result here rules something out.

## 8. Analysis

In R. All models fitted with `lme4` or `ordinal`.

**Primary.**

```r
glmer(actionable ~ exposed + year_c + (1 | journal),
      family = binomial, data = d,
      control = glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa"))
```

Article type does not enter the model because section 4.2 excludes the type that mattered.

**Secondary, preregistered.**

```r
clmm(grade ~ exposed + year_c + (1 | journal), data = d)
```

**Sensitivity analyses, all preregistered here, all reported whatever they show.**

1. Supplementary material graded actionable rather than declarative.
2. Publisher-typed extraction routes only, excluding title-matched routes.
3. Journal as a fixed effect, restricted to journals contributing at least 10 articles in each
   arm, as a within-journal estimate that does not rely on the random-effect assumption.
4. Excluding any article authored by the author of this project.
5. Excluding the `repro` block from the comparison arm, since reproducibility and data sharing
   are adjacent subjects and their separation is the weakest part of the exposure definition.
6. Restricted to fully open-access journals, added after probe P2 found that PMC coverage
   differs by 20 points between arms. Within a fully open-access journal every article is in
   PMC, so the differential selection that P2 exposed cannot operate. This is a post-probe
   addition and is labeled as one.

**Missing data.** Year is present for every PubMed record. Journal is present for every record.
No imputation is performed and no analysis drops a record for missingness; records are excluded
only by section 4.2.

**Multiplicity.** There is one primary comparison. The secondary and the five sensitivity
analyses are not corrected for multiplicity and are not used to claim significance; they exist
to show whether the primary estimate is fragile.

## 9. Threats, and what happened to each

A threat that is disclosed rather than removed is acceptable. A threat that is silently absent
is not. Every design choice made after seeing a number is listed, because R01 was exploratory
and anything carried forward from it makes a later choice suspect unless it is named.

| threat | what was done | removed or disclosed |
|---|---|---|
| Grading rule was developed on R01's data | Rule frozen at the pinned commit and applied unchanged to a sample it has never seen | Removed |
| Extractor read papers' own results as their disclosure | Restricted to `<front>` and `<back>`; the artifact and its cost are documented in R01 | Removed |
| Repository regex matched inside longer words | Word boundaries added before pinning | Removed |
| Exposure is a query, not a judgment of subject | Kept deliberately, for reproducibility; misclassification is toward the null | Disclosed |
| Comparison arm blocks chosen after seeing R01 | `report`, `reg` and `repro` were picked because they are the other large qualified blocks, not because of any result; sensitivity 5 drops `repro` as the most arguable | Disclosed |
| PMC coverage is not random | Not fixable in this design | Disclosed |
| **PMC coverage differs by arm**, 77.7 percent exposed against 57.8 percent comparison | Found by probe P2, not anticipated when the design was written. Arms are differentially selected into the analysis set and the direction favors the hypothesis. Reported as a primary limitation; a sensitivity restricted to journals that are fully open access is added as analysis 6 | Disclosed, not removed |
| Screening depends on a model | Gated on kappa against a human reference standard, with a stated fallback | Disclosed |
| Comparison-arm rate of 0.205 comes from R01 | Used for planning only; the realized rate is computed in probe P4 and is what the power calculation uses | Disclosed |

## 10. The probes that must run before this is a protocol

Each probe names the number it produces, the section that number goes into, what result would
change the design, and its cost. Nothing here is an argument; each one is a count.

| probe | computes | goes to | what would change the design | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** done | Records retrieved by the four qualified blocks, after removing every tier A PMID | 4.1 | An available count far below 727 for `datashare` makes the confirmatory arm underpowered and the design moves to a prospective re-harvest instead | ~10 min, cached |
| **P2** done | Proportion of P1 records carrying a PMCID | 4.2, 7 | Below about 40 percent and the realized exposed count falls under 150, at which point this is no better powered than R01 and is not worth running | minutes |

| **P7** outstanding, needs the author | Grading agreement: Cohen's kappa of the pinned rule against 150 human-graded statements | 6.3 | Below 0.60 abandons the rule, per 6.3 | 150 statements to label |

**P6 and P7 need a person, and that person is the author, not a model.** A model grading a
sample to validate a rule another model wrote is not a reference standard, and R01 already
shows what that is worth: the 40 statements graded by the model that wrote the rule found a
real defect and still left the rule's agreement with a human unknown. The two CSVs are
produced with empty label columns and handed over.

P6 may use a single model while the second reviewer is quota-blocked. Measuring an instrument
is not reviewing a study, so the two-reviewer rule does not apply to it; it applies to sections
under review, and it is not waived for those.

## 11. Deviations

Any departure from this protocol is recorded in a `DEVIATIONS` section appended to the result
page, stating what changed, when, why, and the estimate before and after where that can be
computed. A deviation is not a failure; concealing one is.

## 12. What this design still cannot do

- **It is not causal.** Researchers who study transparency and researchers who practice it may
  be the same people, and the design cannot separate selection from influence.
- **PMC coverage is not random.** Open-access availability correlates with transparency
  practice, so both arms lean transparent. The direction of bias on a *comparison* between arms
  is not obvious and is not claimed to be toward the null.
- **The qualified frame is lossy.** The study-of-literature qualifier cut the raw `datashare`
  block from 15418 to 754, and a meta-research paper describing its sample as "we examined 300
  trials" carries none of the qualifier phrases and is absent from the frame. The sample is a
  high-precision slice of the field, not the field.
- **It tests association in one direction only.** A finding that transparency-topic papers
  share more does not tell us whether writing about transparency changes behavior or whether
  people who already share choose to write about it.

# Debugging

You are a hypothesis-driven debugger. Two disciplines apply regardless of language, runtime, or whether you have source:

1. **Runtime truth beats code reading.** Every claim about why the bug happens must come from observed state — never from a plausible story spun from reading code.
2. **Leave no trace.** Debugging creates artifacts. Every artifact is journaled and removed before you call the task done.

The rest of this file is a map. **The knowledge is in `references/`.** This file cannot teach you how to debug — it can only tell you which reference will, for your exact situation.

| Rust (cargo, tokio, panics) | 📖 **[references/runtimes/rust.md](references/runtimes/rust.md)** | Release builds strip symbols. Tokio tasks need `tokio-console`. The borrow checker makes `dbg!` the faster tool most of the time. |

| Native binary / stripped C/C++ / no source | 📖 **[references/runtimes/native-binary.md](references/runtimes/native-binary.md)** | The workflow (triage → dynamic → static → scripted repro) is counterintuitive if you've never done it. `strings -n 8` silently drops short interpolations like `${x}` — read bytes directly for any extraction that matters. macOS adds SIP / Mach-O / lldb specifics that don't apply on Linux. |
| **Bundled-app binary** (Bun SEA, Node SEA, Deno compile, pkg, nexe, Electron, Tauri, PyInstaller) | 📖 **[references/runtimes/bundled-js-binary.md](references/runtimes/bundled-js-binary.md)** | These look like Mach-O / ELF but their *high-level* source is recoverable with the right per-bundler tool — Ghidra is overkill. Source-format reality varies: Bun/pkg/nexe/Electron-asar are usually plaintext; Node SEA with code-cache, PyInstaller `.pyc`, and Deno eszip need extra tooling; Tauri's Rust core still needs native-binary.md. Workflow: identify bundler → locate bundle → extract with the bundler-specific tool → grep. |

**If you cannot honestly say you just opened the reference for your runtime, open it now.**

> 🚨 **Native binary vs bundled binary — check before committing**: `file ./target` calls them both Mach-O / ELF. The 30-second discriminator is `du -h ./target` (50 MB+ suspect bundled) plus `strings -n 12 ./target | rg -iE 'bun|node_modules|webpack|esbuild|deno|pkg/lib|electron|pyinstaller|nexe|NODE_SEA_FUSE|tauri'`. If hits → bundled-js-binary.md. If clean → native-binary.md.

|---|---|---|

## Non-Negotiable Safety Invariants

<safety>
1. **Runtime state is the only source of truth.** A hypothesis without an observed value is a guess. Do not fix guesses.
2. **Every debug artifact is journaled before it is created.** Journal-then-modify, not modify-then-remember-maybe.
3. **Never ship a fix without a failing-first test.** Red→green transition required, or the fix is unverified.
4. **Never declare done on type-check/compile alone.** Types catch declaration bugs. Only running the actual user scenario catches the actual user bug.
5. **Never ask the user a question that runtime evidence can already answer.** Escalation is for genuine ambiguity.
6. **Never silently swallow errors while debugging.** If the system swallows errors, that is often the bug itself. Make them loud temporarily; restore at cleanup.
7. **Never `git commit` from inside this skill.** Commits belong to `/git-master` after the user confirms the fix.
8. **Never attach without having read the runtime reference.** The gate rule.
</safety>

# Review Work - 5-Agent Parallel Review Orchestrator

Launch 5 specialized sub-agents in parallel to review completed implementation work from every angle. All 5 must pass for the review to pass. If even ONE fails, the review fails.

When `review-work` is used as a final implementation, PR, or `$start-work`
gate, it is blocking. A timeout, missing deliverable, ack-only response,
explicit `BLOCKED:`, or inconclusive lane is not a pass. Treat that lane as
failed, investigate the underlying uncertainty with the `debugging` skill when
runtime behavior may be wrong, fix with evidence, and rerun the affected lane
before claiming completion, creating or handing off a PR, or merging.

After each lane reaches PASS, immediately append a durable task-evidence record
to the active ledger with the lane name, exact full commit SHA, PASS verdict,
and report artifact/source. Before reusing coverage after continuation or
compaction, re-read that record and require the exact lane/SHA pair. Memory,
chat history, or an unstamped report is not coverage; a new commit requires
fresh applicable lane records.

A rejecting lane must name its blockers inline in its final message — each
blocker cites the violated goal criterion or requirement plus an evidence
pointer. A bare REJECT/FAIL token without findings is not a verdict; treat it
as an inconclusive lane (one bounded respawn, then record it inconclusive with
that reason).

When reviewing a PR or branch, collect diff, file contents, and verification
results from a dedicated review worktree attached to that branch. Never
checkout, test, or edit the review branch in the main worktree.

Review evidence must be safe to share. Redact or mask secrets and sensitive
user data before including evidence in logs, PR bodies, or handoffs. Never
include raw tokens, credentials, auth headers, cookies, API keys, env dumps,
private logs, or PII; summarize with lengths, hashes, and short non-sensitive
prefixes when identity is needed.

```
<original_goal>
{GOAL}
</original_goal>

<constraints>
{CONSTRAINTS}
</constraints>

<changed_files>
{CHANGED_FILES}
</changed_files>

<run_command>
{RUN_COMMAND - how to start the application, or "unknown" if not determined}
</run_command>

You are a QA engineer. Your job is to RUN the application and verify it works through hands-on testing. You do not review code - you test behavior.

If the orchestrator already ran the `visual-qa` dual-oracle gate on this same build, consume that verdict instead of re-running it - your lane covers hands-on behavior the visual gate does not.

MANDATORY PROCESS (follow in order):

### Step 1: Scenario Brainstorm

Before touching the app, write down EVERY test scenario you can think of. Be exhaustive. Think about:

- **Happy paths**: The primary use cases this implementation enables. What's the main thing the user wanted to do?
- **Boundary conditions**: Empty inputs, maximum-length inputs, zero values, negative numbers, special characters, unicode, very large datasets.
- **Error paths**: Invalid inputs, network failures, missing files, permission denied, timeout conditions.
- **Regression scenarios**: Existing features that touch the same code paths. Things that worked before and must still work.
- **State transitions**: What happens when you do things out of order? Rapid repeated actions? Concurrent usage?
- **UX scenarios** (if applicable): Layout on different sizes, keyboard navigation, screen reader compatibility, loading states, error messages.
- **Integration points**: Does this feature interact with external services, databases, or other modules? Test those boundaries.

Write each scenario as a one-liner with expected behavior. Aim for 15-30 scenarios minimum.

### Step 2: Scenario Augmentation

Review your scenario list with fresh eyes. For each scenario, ask:
- "What could go wrong here that I haven't considered?"
- "What would a malicious or careless user do?"
- "What environmental conditions could affect this?" (disk full, slow network, expired tokens)
```

# Partial Runtime Evidence — When You Cannot Execute the Real Operation

Read this when **runtime truth beats code reading** is in conflict with **you cannot run the actual operation**.

The skill's first invariant is "runtime state is the only source of truth." But sometimes the only state you can produce is a *partial* observation — the real call requires paid credits, a hardware device you don't have, network access through a corporate proxy, a production secret, or a customer dataset.

**Partial runtime evidence is still runtime evidence.** This reference tells you which partial signals to harvest and how to combine them so the conclusion is defensible.

---

## When this applies

Use this reference when ALL are true:

1. The bug or extraction question requires runtime confirmation (per skill invariant #1).
2. You attempted the obvious "just run it" path and it failed for reasons unrelated to the bug:
   - 401/402/403 from a paid API
   - "device not found" / "permission denied" / SIP block
   - Production-only credentials
   - Network isolation (air-gapped, behind VPN you don't have)
   - Time-of-day or quota limits
3. **Mocking the entire system** would defeat the verification — you specifically need evidence about how the *real* code behaves, not a stub.

If only #1 and #2 are true and you can mock cleanly, just mock and proceed. This file is for cases where mocking would invalidate the answer.

---

## The hierarchy of partial evidence (strongest first)

When you cannot capture the full outbound payload + full response, capture as much as possible from this list. **Evidence further down the list has more inference; evidence higher up is closer to ground truth.**

### Tier 1 — Pre-send / post-receive logs (best partial evidence)

The system you're investigating builds a request, then sends it. If the build step logs the assembled request **before** transmission, that log is ground truth for everything except the wire-level bytes (TLS, headers added by HTTP library, etc.).

```bash
# Maximize debug logging
APP_DEBUG=1 APP_LOG_LEVEL=debug APP_LOG_FILE=/tmp/trace.log ./target -x "minimal valid input" 2>&1 | head -200
```

Look for log lines like:
- `Building request: model=X, params={...}`
- `[provider] payload: {...}`
- `Sending to <url>: <serialized body>`

**Strength**: 95% of ground truth. Missing only wire-level transformations.

### Tier 2 — Local interception via proxy / shim

Run the real binary against a local proxy that records and (optionally) returns a canned response.

```bash
# mitmproxy approach
mitmproxy --listen-host 127.0.0.1 --listen-port 8888 --mode regular &
HTTPS_PROXY=http://127.0.0.1:8888 SSL_CERT_FILE=~/.mitmproxy/mitmproxy-ca-cert.pem ./target ...
# Now mitmproxy logs the actual TLS-decrypted request
```

```bash
# DYLD_INSERT_LIBRARIES / LD_PRELOAD shim approach
# Wrap the network call to log payload, return a fake 200
# See pwntools.md for shim examples
```

**Strength**: Wire-level ground truth, but requires the target to honor your proxy / preload.

### Tier 3 — Static extraction × runtime fingerprint cross-check

When you cannot send a request at all, you can still cross-check static analysis with whatever the binary does that *doesn't* require the real call:

- The binary builds the request — even if sending fails, the build step ran. Trace it (Tier 1).
- The binary writes a state file or cache — read it.
- The binary emits version-specific User-Agent strings; verify they match your static extraction.
- The binary's `--help` or `--version` output reveals build metadata; verify model lists / feature flags.

**Strength**: Disjoint evidence sources confirming the same fact. Two independent partial signals that agree are nearly as strong as one full observation.

### Tier 4 — Contrastive runtime under different inputs

If you can run with input variant A but not B, run A and reason about B from code:

```bash
# A: minimal trial input — works for free tier
./target --action=read --resource=local-file
# B: full inference call — paid tier required, blocked
# But the request-building code is shared between A and B!
# Capture A's logs, then inspect the code path for B and verify only the model/endpoint diff.
```

**Strength**: Confirms shared code paths; remaining gap is only the difference between A and B.

### Tier 5 — Vendor-published API logs / dashboard

If the operation succeeded earlier (before quota ran out, before access was revoked), the vendor's dashboard / audit log may show the request. Lower fidelity but still observed behavior.

**Strength**: Real wire data, but often summarized — token counts, status codes, no payload bodies.

### Tier 6 — Pure code reading with peer review

If literally none of the above is available, read the code carefully and submit it to **one Oracle for skeptical review** (see "Verification Oracle" below). This is the weakest tier and you must explicitly mark conclusions as "unverified" in the journal.

---

## How to combine partial signals

A defensible conclusion **prefers two independent signals from different tiers**, with one exception: a complete Tier 2 wire-level capture is wire-level ground truth and can stand alone for request-shape claims (because the wire bytes are exactly what the remote received). For *behavioral* claims (what the system does next, what state it stores, what side effects it produces), still combine with another signal.

| Available evidence | Defensibility |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 + Tier 1 (same log, different lines) | weak — single source |
| Tier 1 + Tier 2 (debug log + proxy capture) | **strong** — independent confirmation |
| Tier 1 + Tier 3 (debug log + version output cross-check) | **strong** — disjoint sources |
| Tier 2 alone (full proxy capture) | strong **for request-shape claims only** — stands alone for "what bytes were sent". Add a second signal for response-handling or state claims. |
| Tier 3 + Tier 4 (cross-check + contrastive run) | medium — both partial |
| Tier 6 alone (code reading only) | **insufficient** — escalate or mark unverified |

Record in the journal:

```markdown
## Partial runtime evidence
### Question being verified
<the specific claim, e.g. "Opus 4.7 default effort is 'high'">

### Available signals
- Tier 1: debug log /tmp/trace.log line 47-49 shows `effort: "high"` ✓
- Tier 3: static extraction of m5T() function returns "high" for smart mode ✓
- Tier 6: code path verified by reading prompt-builder.js ✓

### Independence assessment
Tier 1 and Tier 3 are independent — the log was emitted by a different
code path than m5T() and would diverge if the static reading were wrong.

### Conclusion
VERIFIED via Tier 1 + Tier 3 agreement. No need to escalate.
```

If you cannot achieve a complete Tier 2 capture **or** two independent non-Tier-6 signals from the table above, **write an explicit note in the deliverable**:

> ⚠️ Partial-evidence finding. The full outbound payload could not be captured because [reason]. The conclusion rests on:
> - [signal A — tier and source]
> - [signal B — tier and source]
> A future verification should attempt [the missing tier] when [condition].

### When to invoke

- Right before declaring an extraction/audit task "done"
- After every significant revision of the deliverable (not after every small edit)
- Maximum 3-4 iterations before escalating to user

| | Oracle Triple (debug) | Verification Oracle (artifact) |

| Goal | Break out of mental box | Catch unsubstantiated claims |

## Common partial-evidence anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Why it fails | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| "It looks right in the code, so it works" | Tier 6 alone, unverified | Add at least one Tier 1-3 signal |
| "I ran it once, didn't error, so it's correct" | Absence of error ≠ presence of correctness | Capture the actual output and verify content |
| "The mock returns the value I wrote, so the code is fine" | Tautology — mock loops back your assumption | Use Tier 2 (proxy) instead, or cross-check with Tier 3 |
| "The vendor's dashboard shows my call worked" | Dashboard often only shows status code, not behavior | Combine with Tier 1 if available |
| "I'll trust the most-recent stack overflow answer" | Code from a different version / context | Verify against the actual binary you have |

"""Probes P1 to P5 for the P01v2 design.

The sibling project's rule: the difference between a design and a protocol is a set of
computed numbers, and a design that asserts those numbers instead of computing them is a
named failure mode. Every number this emits goes into a numbered section of
`proposals/P01v2-advocacy-and-practice-protocol.qmd` and is written there from
`out/probes.json`, never retyped.

P1  frame size after removing every tier A PMID
P2  PMCID coverage of what survives
P3  full-text rate and extraction route distribution   (needs extract; separate step)
P4  grade distribution on the comparison arm           (needs P3)
P5  detectable odds ratio from the realized counts     (computed in R, needs P4)

Usage: python3 studies/P01v2-confirmatory/probes.py
"""

import collections
import json
import os
import sys

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
ROOT = os.path.dirname(os.path.dirname(HERE))
LIT = os.path.join(ROOT, "data", "lit")
OUT = os.path.join(HERE, "out")

EXPOSED_BLOCK = "datashare"
COMPARISON_BLOCKS = ("report", "reg", "repro")

# Publication types excluded by section 4.2. An article with no data of its own belongs to a
# different population, and its absence of a statement is not evidence about sharing behavior.
EXCLUDED_PUBTYPES = ("Comment", "Editorial", "Letter", "News", "Biography",
                     "Historical Article", "Retracted Publication", "Retraction of Publication")


def load(path):
    with open(path, encoding="utf8") as fh:
        return [json.loads(line) for line in fh]


def main():
    tier_a = load(os.path.join(LIT, "records.jsonl"))
    frame = load(os.path.join(LIT, "frame-P01v2.jsonl"))

    # The independence condition, applied first so no later step can reintroduce a record.
    # PMID is the key; a frame record without one cannot be checked against tier A and is
    # dropped rather than assumed new.
    tier_a_pmids = {r["pmid"] for r in tier_a if r["pmid"]}
    no_pmid = [r for r in frame if not r["pmid"]]
    overlap = [r for r in frame if r["pmid"] in tier_a_pmids]
    indep = [r for r in frame if r["pmid"] and r["pmid"] not in tier_a_pmids]

    def arm_of(r):
        blocks = set(r["found_by"])
        if EXPOSED_BLOCK in blocks:
            return "exposed"
        if blocks & set(COMPARISON_BLOCKS):
            return "comparison"
        return None

    eligible, excluded_type, excluded_lang = [], 0, 0
    for r in indep:
        if any(p in EXCLUDED_PUBTYPES for p in r["pubtypes"]):
            excluded_type += 1
            continue
        if r["language"] and r["language"].lower() not in ("eng", "english", ""):
            excluded_lang += 1
            continue
        eligible.append(r)

    arms = collections.Counter(arm_of(r) for r in eligible)
    with_pmcid = collections.Counter(arm_of(r) for r in eligible if r["pmcid"])

    # Tier A's own PMC rate, as the reference the design's discarded assumption came from.
    tier_a_pmc = sum(1 for r in tier_a if r["pmcid"]) / len(tier_a)

    p = {

            "frame_records": len(frame),
            "dropped_no_pmid": len(no_pmid),
            "dropped_overlap_with_tier_a": len(overlap),
            "independent": len(indep),
            "excluded_pubtype": excluded_type,
            "excluded_language": excluded_lang,
            "eligible_before_screening": len(eligible),
            "eligible_exposed": arms["exposed"],
            "eligible_comparison": arms["comparison"],
            "by_block": dict(collections.Counter(
                b for r in eligible for b in r["found_by"])),
        },
        "P2": {
            "with_pmcid_exposed": with_pmcid["exposed"],
            "with_pmcid_comparison": with_pmcid["comparison"],

            "pmcid_rate_comparison": round(
                with_pmcid["comparison"] / max(arms["comparison"], 1), 4),
            "tier_a_pmcid_rate_for_reference": round(tier_a_pmc, 4),
        },

# IMPORTANT: the comparison arm here is NOT screened. Screening needs the P6 pilot, which needs
# a human reference standard. Everything below is an estimate on an unscreened frame.
#
# A first version of this comment asserted that the unscreened comparison rate is an UPPER bound
# on the screened one, reasoning that primary studies carry journal-mandated statements. The
# probe says the opposite and the probe is right: the unscreened comparison rate is 0.038 while
# R01's rate among screened meta-research was 0.205. Screening removes primary studies, which
# deposit far less often than meta-research does, so screening RAISES the comparison rate and
# the unscreened figure is a LOWER bound.
#
# That matters because the detectable effect depends on the baseline, so power is computed

# comes out backwards, and only computing it shows that.

# Detectable p1 at 80 percent power with unequal allocation. power.prop.test assumes equal
# group sizes and would understate what 520 against 3435 can do, so the two-proportion normal
# approximation is solved directly for p1.
detectable_p1 <- function(n1, n0, p0, power = 0.8, alpha = 0.05) {
  za <- qnorm(1 - alpha / 2); zb <- qnorm(power)
  f <- function(p1) {
    pbar <- (p1 * n1 + p0 * n0) / (n1 + n0)
    num <- abs(p1 - p0) - za * sqrt(pbar * (1 - pbar) * (1 / n1 + 1 / n0))
    den <- sqrt(p1 * (1 - p1) / n1 + p0 * (1 - p0) / n0)
    num / den - zb
  }
  uniroot(f, c(p0 + 1e-4, 0.999))$root
}

or_of <- function(p1, p0) (p1 / (1 - p1)) / (p0 / (1 - p0))
p1_det <- detectable_p1(n1, n0, p0)

# Power across plausible screened comparison rates. Screening shrinks both arms, so the counts
# are scaled by an assumed yield and the yield is varied too; both are assumptions and are
# reported as such rather than buried.
grid <- expand.grid(p0_s = c(0.038, 0.10, 0.15, 0.205, 0.25), yield = c(0.5, 0.7, 1.0))
grid$n1_s <- round(n1 * grid$yield); grid$n0_s <- round(n0 * grid$yield)
grid$det_or <- mapply(function(a, b, c) round(or_of(detectable_p1(a, b, c), c), 2),
                      grid$n1_s, grid$n0_s, grid$p0_s)
cat("\n== P5 sensitivity: detectable OR by screened baseline rate and screening yield ==\n")
print(reshape(grid[, c("p0_s", "yield", "det_or")], idvar = "p0_s",
              timevar = "yield", direction = "wide"), row.names = FALSE)

cat("\n== P5 ==\n")
cat("exposed n =", n1, " comparison n =", n0, "\n")
cat("comparison actionable rate (UNSCREENED) =", round(p0, 4), "\n")
cat("observed exposed actionable rate (UNSCREENED) =", round(p1_obs, 4), "\n")

# The grading rule for a data availability statement. Pinned by the P01v2 protocol.
#
# This file exists so the rule is never typed twice. It is sourced by
# studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/R/analyze.R and by studies/P01v2-confirmatory/R/probes.R,
# and the sibling project's operational rule applies: any number or decision appearing in two
# places is emitted from one and asserted back, never copied.
#
# Editing this file changes a pinned instrument. Do not, without recording a deviation on the
# protocol and re-running both studies.

REPO <- paste0(
  "\\bosf\\.io|\\bzenodo\\b|\\bdryad\\b|\\bfigshare\\b|\\bdataverse\\b|",
  "\\bmendeley data\\b|\\bopendata\\b|\\bgithub\\.com|\\bgitlab\\.com|\\bbitbucket\\b|",
  "\\bcodeocean\\b|\\bopenneuro\\b|\\bdbgap\\b|\\bgeo accession\\b|\\barrayexpress\\b|",
  "\\bsequence read archive\\b|\\bharvard dataverse\\b|\\bborealis\\b|\\b4tu\\b|",
  "\\bpangaea\\b|\\bdatacite\\b|\\bre3data\\b"
)
ON_REQUEST <- paste0(
  "available (from|upon|on)( the)? (corresponding )?(author|authors|first author|request)|",
  "on reasonable request|upon reasonable request|available on request|",
  "by request to|contact the corresponding author"
)
NOT_APPLICABLE <- paste0(
  "^n/?a\\.?$|no datasets? (were|was) (generated|analys|analyz)|",
  "not applicable|no (new )?data (were|was) (generated|created)|",
  "no additional data|does not (have|contain) any (additional )?data"
)
IN_ARTICLE <- paste0(
  "included (in|within) (this|the) (published )?article|",
  "within the (manuscript|article|paper)|",
  "provided within the manuscript|in the supplementary|as supplementary"
)
DOI <- "10\\.[0-9]{4,9}/[^\\s,;]+"
URL <- "https?://[^\\s,;\\)]+"

grade_one <- function(s) {
  if (!nzchar(str_trim(s))) return("none")
  x <- str_to_lower(str_squish(s))
  # Strip the section heading the extractor keeps, so "Data availability" itself is not text.
  x <- str_remove(x, "^(data|code|software)[^a-z]*availability[^a-z]*(statement)?")
  x <- str_remove(x, "^availability of (the )?(data|code|materials)[^a-z]*")
  x <- str_trim(x)
  if (!nzchar(x)) return("declarative")
  # A named repository is a deposit and outranks everything below it.
  if (str_detect(x, REPO)) return("actionable")
  if (str_detect(x, ON_REQUEST)) return("declarative")
  if (str_detect(x, NOT_APPLICABLE)) return("declarative")
  # A DOI or URL counts only when it is not the article's own DOI or the journal's own site.
  loc <- c(str_extract_all(x, DOI)[[1]], str_extract_all(x, URL)[[1]])
  loc <- loc[!str_detect(loc, "doi\\.org/10\\.[0-9]{4,9}/$")]
  if (length(loc) > 0) return("actionable")
  if (str_detect(x, IN_ARTICLE)) return("declarative")
  "declarative"
}

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""P01 harvest: pull the data availability statement out of every corpus record in PMC.

Python does the retrieval and the text extraction; every decision about what a statement
means is made in R, in `R/analyze.R`, so that the grading rule and the model live together
and can be read as one argument.

The output is one CSV row per record with a PMCID, carrying the raw statement text and the
JATS route it was found by. Nothing is graded here.

Usage: python3 studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/extract.py [--refresh]
"""

import argparse
import csv
import hashlib
import json
import os
import re
import sys
from xml.etree import ElementTree as ET

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
ROOT = os.path.dirname(os.path.dirname(HERE))
sys.path.insert(0, os.path.join(ROOT, "build", "lit"))
from search import get, EUTILS, EMAIL, TOOL, _text  # noqa: E402

CORPUS = os.path.join(ROOT, "data", "lit", "records-labelled.jsonl")
CACHE = os.path.join(HERE, "data", "_cache")

# The places a data availability statement lives in JATS, in the order they are tried, and
# only within <front> and <back>. A publisher picks one and is consistent, but the corpus
# spans hundreds of publishers, so all of them are needed. `route` is written to the output
# because a result that depends on which route a publisher uses is a result about publishers,
# and the route has to be visible to check that.
#
# Two things this deliberately does not do. It does not fall back to a free-text search of
# the body for "data are available", because a sentence in a discussion is not a statement.
# And it does not treat the absence of a section as proof the authors shared nothing; it is
# proof the article carries no statement, which is the outcome actually being measured.
TITLE_RX = re.compile(
    r"(data|code|software)\s+(and\s+(code|materials?|software)\s+)?availability"
    r"|availability\s+of\s+(the\s+)?(data|code|materials)"
    r"|data\s+sharing\s+statement"
    r"|data\s+access(ibility)?\s+statement",
    re.I,
)


def statement_from(art):
    """The statement text and the route it came from, or ("", "none").

    Only <front> and <back> are searched, never <body>, and that restriction is the whole
    correctness argument. A first version walked the entire article and produced a result
    that was an artifact: a meta-research paper ABOUT data availability carries sections
    called "Data availability over time" and "Extraction of data availability status" in its
    own methods and results, and reading those as the paper's own statement graded the paper
    actionable because they are full of repository URLs. Four of the 43 exposed records were
    contaminated this way, and the exposed arm hit the fuzzy title routes three times more
    often than the unexposed arm, which is what the bias looks like from outside.

    A data availability statement is front or back matter. A numbered section of the
    narrative is the paper's argument, not its disclosure. One publisher even tags a numbered
    methods section `sec-type="data-availability"` (PMC8441137), so the type attribute alone
    does not save this; the location does. PMC11078384 shows the fix is not merely subtractive:
    its real statement is in <front> and was being shadowed by its own results section.
    """
    for part in ("front", "back"):
        parent = art.find(part)
        if parent is None:
            continue
        # 1. A section or notes block explicitly typed by the publisher.
        for tag in ("sec", "notes"):
            for node in parent.iter(tag):
                t = (node.get("sec-type") or node.get("notes-type") or "").lower()
                if "data-availability" in t or "data-access" in t or t == "availability":
                    return _text(node), f"{part}-{tag}-type"
        # 2. A section or notes block whose title says what it is.
        for tag in ("sec", "notes"):
            for node in parent.iter(tag):
                title = node.find("title")
                if title is not None and TITLE_RX.search(_text(title)):
                    return _text(node), f"{part}-{tag}-title"
        # 3. A custom-meta pair, used by a few publishers for the same purpose.
        for cm in parent.iter("custom-meta"):
            name = _text(cm.find("meta-name"))
            if name and TITLE_RX.search(name):
                return _text(cm.find("meta-value")), f"{part}-custom-meta"
    return "", "none"


def has_body(art):
    """Whether PMC actually holds the full text, as opposed to metadata with an abstract.

    A record with no <body> cannot have a statement extracted and must be excluded rather
    than counted as a record without one. Conflating the two would put every abstract-only
    deposit in the "no statement" arm and bias the whole result.
    """
    body = art.find("body")
    return body is not None and len(_text(body)) > 500


def fetch(pmcids, refresh=False):
    os.makedirs(CACHE, exist_ok=True)
    size = 20
    for i in range(0, len(pmcids), size):
        batch = pmcids[i:i + size]
        digest = hashlib.sha1(",".join(batch).encode()).hexdigest()[:12]
        path = os.path.join(CACHE, f"pmc_{i:06d}_{digest}.xml")
        if os.path.exists(path) and os.path.getsize(path) > 500 and not refresh:
            xml = open(path, encoding="utf8", errors="ignore").read()
        else:
            xml = get(f"{EUTILS}/efetch.fcgi",
                      data=f"db=pmc&id={','.join(batch)}&retmode=xml"
                           f"&email={EMAIL}&tool={TOOL}")
            open(path, "w", encoding="utf8").write(xml)
        print(f"  {min(i + size, len(pmcids))}/{len(pmcids)}", end="\r", file=sys.stderr)
        yield xml
    print(file=sys.stderr)

    ap = argparse.ArgumentParser()

This project studies transparency in research. It would be indefensible for the project itself
to be opaque, so every query, every count and every scope decision is recorded here, including
the ones that went wrong.

## Databases and dates

PubMed and PMC, through the NCBI E-utilities. PMC is searched as well as PubMed because PMC
carries records PubMed does not index: author manuscripts, some non-MEDLINE journals, and
deposited preprints. Searches were run on 2026-09-16. No language, date or article-type limit
was applied.

The harvester is `build/lit/search.py`. It is the only route by which a record enters the
corpus, and it writes a machine-readable log of every query it issued to
`data/lit/search-log.json`.

## The two tiers

The field can be searched two ways and they retrieve different things.

**Tier A** is the field naming itself: a paper that describes its own work as meta-research,
metascience, research on research, or a meta-epidemiological study. Precision is close to
total, because no other kind of paper uses those words about itself.

**Tier B** is the field by subject matter: reporting quality, data sharing, retraction,
registration, peer review, and the rest. Tier B retrieves two populations that no boolean query
separates. A search for `"data sharing"` returns surveys of how often trials share data
alongside trials that happened to share theirs, and only the first kind is meta-research.

**Only tier A has been harvested.** Tier B has been sized and the counts are published below,
but nothing from it is in the corpus. That decision is revisited once the tier A map has been
read.

## Tier A: the query

Each phrase is searched in title, abstract and author keyword fields, and the phrases are
combined with OR.

```
"meta-research"      "metaresearch"           "meta research"
"metascience"        "meta-science"           "research on research"
"science of science" "meta-epidemiological"   "meta-epidemiology"
```

| database | hits | records retrieved |
|---|---|---|
| PubMed | 1805 | 1805 |
| PMC | 694 | 694 |

Deduplicated on PMID, then DOI, then PMCID, then normalized title: **1830 unique records**.
The overlap is large, as expected; 669 PMC records were already present from PubMed and 25 were
new.

### The 189588

The `reg` block first returned 189588 records, five sixths of everything tier B retrieved, from
the single phrase `"trial registration"`. The obvious explanation was wrong. The phrase was not
being broken into words and ANDed by PubMed; it is phrase-indexed, and the quoted form returns
fewer records than `trial AND registration` does. The real cause is that **Trial registration**
is the label of a section in a structured abstract, so every registered trial in PubMed carries
the phrase verbatim. It identifies trials that were registered and never studies of
registration.

The phrase has been removed from the block. `build/lit/search.py` records why, next to the
block it was removed from, so it is not reintroduced by someone who only sees that the block
looks thin.

`"registered report"` was removed for a related reason: the singular returns 5806 records and
the plural 192, because the singular also matches any sentence about a report that was
registered. Only the plural is kept.

### The qualifier

For the contaminated blocks, ANDing the topic with a set of phrases that name the object of
study separates a study of the literature from a study of patients. A study of data sharing
examines published articles; a trial that shared its data examines people.

```
"published articles"  "published studies"    "published trials"
"journal articles"    "publications"         "sample of articles"
"cross-sectional study of"                   "sample of trials"
"we searched PubMed"  "we searched MEDLINE"  "we searched Embase"
"research articles"   "abstracts of"         "meta-epidemiological"
```

It is effective and it is lossy. A meta-research paper that describes its sample as "we
examined 300 trials" carries none of these phrases and would be lost. Both numbers are
therefore recorded, so any qualified corpus is reported against the raw count it was drawn
from rather than presented as the whole field.

## Retrieval limits, and why nothing was silently truncated

NCBI returns at most 9999 identifiers for one query. The sibling project this harvester is
adapted from partitions an oversized query by year and then by month and stops there, so a
single month above the ceiling truncates without saying so. Meta-research blocks are large and
concentrated in recent years, which is where that would happen.

The partitioner here bisects the publication-date range recursively until every part is under
the ceiling. When even a single day exceeds it, the day is recorded in `gaps` in the search log
and printed as `TRUNCATED`, rather than passed off as complete. The tier A harvest needed no
partitioning: both queries were under the ceiling.

## Labelling

Topic labels are assigned in `build/lit/describe.py` by matching the tier B phrase vocabulary
against each record's title, abstract and author keywords. Matching is case-insensitive,
treats a hyphen and a space as equivalent, as PubMed does, and requires whole-token boundaries
so that `spin` does not match `spinal`.

Two vocabularies are involved and conflating them caused the one error this project has so far
published. The **search** vocabulary is tuned for precision against all of PubMed, where a
phrase as broad as `"peer review"` retrieves every paper that mentions having been peer
reviewed. The **labelling** vocabulary applies inside a corpus that is already meta-research,
where that same narrowness only hides topics: the search phrases labelled peer review on 11
records while 159 of them mention it, and generative AI on 1 while 66 mention a language model.
A conclusion drawn from those counts, that the field does not study its own quality control,
was published on the landscape page and was false.

Broad labelling phrases now live in `LABEL_EXTRA` in `build/lit/describe.py`, applied only to
records already retrieved and never to a query. The corrected counts are on
[the landscape](landscape.qmd), which also states what the earlier version claimed.

A label means the record uses a topic's vocabulary. It does not mean a human, or a model,
judged the record to be about that topic. No model has read any record in this corpus. Records
can carry several labels; 1819 of 1830 carry at least one and 11 carry none.

## What this corpus is not

- **It is not the meta-research literature.** It is the part of it that names itself. Work
  published before roughly 2015, when the vocabulary settled, largely does not appear even when
  it is unmistakably meta-research by subject.
- **It is not screened.** Every record retrieved by the tier A query is in, including editorials,
  comments and letters. Publication types are reported on the landscape page so a reader can see
  how much of the corpus that is.
- **It carries no preprints beyond what PMC deposits.** Europe PMC indexes preprint servers
  under a source PubMed has no equivalent for. Adding that supplement is a known gap, not an
  oversight.
- **Nothing here has been peer reviewed yet.** The review process is described in
  [the pipeline](index.qmd#the-pipeline) and has not run.

find_p2 <- function(target_n1) {
  f <- function(p2) {
    if (p2 <= 0 || p2 >= 1 || abs(p2 - p0) < 1e-4) return(Inf)
    power.prop.test(p1 = p0, p2 = p2, sig.level = 0.05, power = 0.8)$n - target_n1
  }

  up

## The four ways to attach

| Method | When to use | Command |
|---|---|---|
| **`breakpoint()` inline** (Python 3.7+) | You can edit the source and restart. Most reliable. | Add `breakpoint()` to source. Run normally. It invokes `pdb` by default. |
| **`python -m pdb <script>`** | No source edit desired. Breaks on entry. | `python -m pdb script.py arg1` |
| **post-mortem `pdb.pm()`** | Exception already happened, you want to inspect state | In an exception-caught REPL: `import pdb; pdb.pm()` after the exception propagates |
| **debugpy (remote / IDE)** | IDE attach, remote host, containerized process | `python -m debugpy --listen 5678 --wait-for-client script.py` then attach from VS Code / PyCharm |

### Prefer `ipdb` or `pudb` over plain `pdb` when available

- **ipdb** — drop-in replacement with tab completion, syntax highlighting. `pip install ipdb`, then `PYTHONBREAKPOINT=ipdb.set_trace` or use `import ipdb; ipdb.set_trace()`.
- **pudb** — full-screen TUI debugger, much faster to navigate stack/locals. `pip install pudb`, then `PYTHONBREAKPOINT=pudb.set_trace`.

### Control `breakpoint()` globally

```bash
# Use ipdb instead of pdb
export PYTHONBREAKPOINT=ipdb.set_trace

# Disable all breakpoint() calls (useful to ship without removing them)
export PYTHONBREAKPOINT=0
```

**Journal this env var** — unset at Phase 9.

## asyncio gotchas

Async is where most Python debug sessions go sideways. Know these before attaching.

### Breakpoints inside coroutines

`breakpoint()` works inside an async function, but stepping into another coroutine from `pdb` is awkward. Two techniques:

```python
async def handler():
    result = await some_async_fn()  # add breakpoint ABOVE, not inside, when possible
    breakpoint()
    return result
```

Inside the breakpoint, to inspect a coroutine without actually advancing time:
```
!import asyncio
!loop = asyncio.get_event_loop()
!task = asyncio.ensure_future(some_async_fn())
# Now inspect task state, don't await it
p task
```

### PYTHONASYNCIODEBUG

Enable before running the process:

```bash
PYTHONASYNCIODEBUG=1 python script.py
```

Surfaces: coroutines that were never awaited, slow callbacks, unhandled task exceptions. **Always turn this on** if the bug is timing- or async-related.

### `asyncio.gather` swallows the first exception

By default, `asyncio.gather(t1, t2)` raises the first exception and cancels the rest. If you need all exceptions, use `gather(..., return_exceptions=True)`.

### Unhandled task exceptions are silent

```python
async def main():
    task = asyncio.create_task(broken_coroutine())
    # If task raises and we never await it, the exception is eaten at gc time
    await asyncio.sleep(10)
```

To catch these, set `loop.set_exception_handler(...)` or upgrade to Python 3.12+ which warns louder by default.

## Sampling profilers for "why is it slow / stuck"

When the problem is performance or a hang (not a crash), don't attach pdb — it alters timing. Use a sampling profiler that attaches to the running process:

```bash
# py-spy — production-safe, zero code change, works on running process
```

## Silent-failure patterns in Python

Add these to Phase 8's silent-failure check:

| Pattern | Why it's silent |
|---|---|
| `except Exception: pass` or `except: pass` | Catches and discards every error including KeyboardInterrupt |
| `logging.exception(...)` in a logger with no handlers | "Logs" but actually writes nowhere |
| `asyncio.create_task(coro)` without storing the task | Task GC'd before completion, exception swallowed |
| `return x.get("key")` where key is missing | Returns None silently, caller often doesn't check |
| `subprocess.run(..., check=False)` with ignored returncode | Non-zero exit treated as success |
| Django `transaction.atomic()` inside a broader `except` | Rolls back silently |
| `contextlib.suppress(Exception)` | Explicit silencer; easy to leave wider than intended |
| `queue.get(block=False)` with `except queue.Empty: pass` | Polling that silently drops the work |

# Phase 0 + 1 — Environment Assessment & Journal Setup

Before a debugger touches anything, you need a map of what's running and a ledger of what you'll touch. Skipping either phase is how debug sessions turn into "why is my repo dirty a week later" sessions.

---

## Phase 0 — Environment Assessment

Map the ground truth before you attach. Attaching the wrong way wastes the first hour.

### 1. Identify the runtime

Read the actual manifest file, don't guess from extensions:

- Python → `pyproject.toml`, `requirements*.txt`, `setup.py`, `uv.lock`, `.python-version`
- Node → `package.json` (check `scripts`, check `engines`, check `type: module`)
- Rust → `Cargo.toml`, `rust-toolchain*`
- Go → `go.mod`, `go.sum`
- Native / mixed → `Makefile`, `CMakeLists.txt`, the binary itself (`file <path>`)

### 2. Load the matching runtime reference

The moment you know the runtime, open `references/runtimes/<runtime>.md`. The commands in this phase (and every phase after) are runtime-specific. The shape of the answers is the same; the commands are not.

### 3. Gather observable environment state

The shape of the answers you need (commands in the runtime reference):

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What binary/interpreter/runtime actually launches the process? | Determines debugger flag plumbing. Wrappers (`tsx`, `poetry run`, `cargo run`, `bun`, supervisor scripts) change how flags propagate. |
| Is there already a debug-relevant port in use, or another instance of the service running? | Either attach to it or kill it deliberately — never silently compete. |
| Are symbols / source maps / debug info present and correct? | This determines whether breakpoints land on the right lines. Compiled-but-not-debug builds, stripped binaries, and incomplete source maps all silently misplace breakpoints. |
| Does the code path require env vars, config files, or auth tokens to reach the bug? | Missing env often produces early-return paths that masquerade as the bug itself. |
| Is there an existing failing test or known repro? | Prefer amplifying an existing repro over inventing one. |
| Are watchers (file watchers, hot reloaders, supervisors) going to restart the process mid-session? | If yes, turn them off before attaching. Restarts drop inspector connections and invalidate breakpoints. |

### 4. Gate check

If any answer is "I'm not sure", you are not ready for Phase 1. Investigate until certain. Guessing here cascades into false-positive hypotheses in Phase 2.

### The journal-then-modify rule

Before any modification to the repo, shell, or system state, append to "Artifacts to revert" first. This one discipline is what prevents debug sessions from becoming git cleanup sessions.

If you catch yourself about to run a command that creates a file, opens a port, or modifies source — stop, journal the intended artifact with its revert command, then run the command. Not the other way around.

### Why a single journal (not scattered TODO comments)

- One `git checkout`, one `rm`, one `tmux kill-session` list — simple Phase 9 walk.
- Survives interruptions. If you get pulled away mid-session, the next agent (or you later) can continue or revert without guessing.
- Prevents the most common failure: leaving `console.log`/`print()`/`dbg!` scattered across the tree.

## What the grades mean

| grade | meaning |
|---|---|
| none | no data availability statement in the article's front or back matter |
| declarative | a statement that shares nothing: on request, within the article, not applicable |
| actionable | a named repository, accession, DOI or URL a reader can follow without asking |

## Two defects found during the run, and what each cost

Both were found by checking the result rather than by the code failing. Neither raised an
error, and the first produced a plausible answer that was partly an artifact.

### The extractor read papers' own results as their statements

A meta-research paper about data availability contains sections called "Data availability over
time" and "Extraction of data availability status" in its own methods and results. The first
extractor walked the whole article, found those, and graded the paper actionable because such
sections are full of repository URLs. Four of the 43 exposed records were contaminated, and the
signature was visible from outside: the exposed arm hit the fuzzy title-matching routes three
times more often than the unexposed arm.

A publisher-assigned `sec-type="data-availability"` does not protect against this. One article
carries that attribute on a numbered methods section. Location does protect against it: a data
availability statement is front or back matter, and a numbered section of the narrative is the
paper's argument rather than its disclosure. Extraction is now restricted to `<front>` and
`<back>`.

The repair was not purely subtractive. One article's real statement sits in `<front>` and was
being shadowed by its own results section.

**Cost: the uncorrected odds ratio was 3.86. After the fix it is higher, not lower.** The
artifact was not what was producing the effect, which is not what was expected when the check
began.

### The grading rule downgraded deposits that also offered data on request

The rule tested for an on-request clause before testing for a repository, on the reasoning that
"available from the corresponding author at an email address" contains something that looks
like a locator and is a refusal. That is right for a bare URL and wrong for a named repository.
One article deposits its code on GitHub and OSF and separately says the raw Scopus data cannot
be released under license; it shared what it could, and the rule called it declarative.

A named repository now outranks a co-occurring on-request clause. Six of 574 statements carry
both, five of them a named repository.

**Cost: all six are in the unexposed arm, so the correction raises the comparison rate and
works against the hypothesis.** The odds ratio fell from 4.67 to 4.40.

## Sensitivity

**Three of these four analyses are post hoc.** An earlier version of this page said each was
"specified for a reason stated before it was run, not selected after seeing it." That was
false and it is corrected here rather than edited away. Only the ordinal model was
preregistered in [P01](../proposals/P01-advocacy-and-practice.qmd). Two of the three
sensitivities below were invented in direct response to defects found during the run, which is
a reasonable thing to do and is not the same thing as having planned them.

| analysis | preregistered | odds ratio | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|
| **primary** | yes | **4.40** | 1.80 to 10.75 |
| ordinal model, all three levels | yes | see above | p = 0.011 |
| publisher-typed routes only, no title matching by us | no, post hoc | 3.03 | 1.12 to 8.23 |
| supplementary material counted as actionable | no, post hoc | 2.58 | 1.14 to 5.85 |
| excluding the author's own papers | no, post hoc | 4.10 | 1.65 to 10.20 |

The publisher-typed row is the one that matters most. The repaired bug lived in the
title-matching routes, so this refits using only statements a publisher explicitly tagged, where no pattern of
ours decides what counts as a statement. The effect is smaller and it survives.

The supplementary-material row relaxes the protocol's choice to grade "included in this published article and
its supplementary information files" as declarative. That choice is defensible rather than
obviously right, since supplementary material is retrievable by anyone with journal access.
Relaxing it moves 95 articles into the actionable arm and roughly halves the effect, which it
does not remove.

## The author's own papers are in this corpus

A tier A search for meta-research retrieves the work of anyone who does meta-research,
including whoever is running the study. Two articles in the analysis set name the author's own
repositories. Both are in the exposed arm and both are graded actionable, so their presence
pushes the estimate in the direction of the hypothesis. Removing them changes the odds ratio
from 4.40 to 4.10. Reported rather than silently dropped.

## What this does not show

- **It is not causal and the design cannot make it so.** A researcher who studies data sharing
  and a researcher who shares data may simply be the same kind of person, or may work in the
  same few journals with the same few policies. The journal random intercept absorbs the
  average journal but not an interaction between a journal's policy and a paper's topic.
- **43 exposed records is thin.** The smallest effect this design could have detected at 80
  percent power was a rise from 20.5 to 48.9 percent. The observed effect is larger than that
  floor, which is why it is visible; an effect half this size would have been missed, and a
  null here would have ruled out very little.
- **The exposure is a vocabulary match, not a judgment.** A paper labelled as a transparency
  topic may mention data sharing once in passing. That misclassification is non-differential
  with respect to the outcome and biases toward the null, so it weakens this finding rather
  than manufacturing it.
- **60 percent PMC coverage is not random.** 742 corpus records have no PMCID and 89 more are
  in PMC as metadata only. Open-access availability is itself correlated with transparency
  practice, so the sample leans transparent. This is stated in the protocol and is not fixable
  within the design.
- **The grading rule has been validated by a model and not yet by a person.** See below.

## Validation still outstanding

The protocol calls for 100 statements graded by hand to measure where the rule disagrees.
`studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/out/validation-sample.csv` holds that sample with an empty
`hand_grade` column, and the agreement will be reported here when it is filled in.

What has happened instead is weaker and is labeled as such: 40 statements were read and graded
by the same model that wrote the rule, which found the second defect above. A model checking
its own rule is not an independent check, and the kappa against a person is not yet known.

# NCBI allows 3 requests/second without an API key.
THROTTLE = 0.36
_last = [0.0]


def get(url, data=None, tries=5):
    for attempt in range(tries):
        wait = THROTTLE - (time.time() - _last[0])
        if wait > 0:
            time.sleep(wait)
        _last[0] = time.time()
        try:
            req = urllib.request.Request(
                url,
                data=data.encode() if data else None,
                headers={"User-Agent": UA, "Accept": "*/*"},
            )
            with urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=120) as r:
                return r.read().decode("utf8", "ignore")
        except Exception as e:  # noqa: BLE001 - transient NCBI failures are routine
            if attempt == tries - 1:
                raise
            print(f"    retry {attempt + 1} after {e}", file=sys.stderr)
            time.sleep(2 ** attempt)
    return ""

def _or(db, variants):
    parts = []
    for v in variants:
        parts.append(f'"{v}"[Title/Abstract]')
        if db == "pubmed":
            parts.append(f'"{v}"[Other Term]')
    return " OR ".join(parts)


def term_for(db, variants, qualified=False):
    """One query string for a block, over title, abstract and author keywords.

    Tier A is never qualified. A paper that calls itself meta-research needs no further
    evidence that research is what it studies, and qualifying it would only lose records.
    """
    t = _or(db, variants)
    return f"({t}) AND ({_or(db, QUALIFIER)})" if qualified else t

def article_ids(art, paths):
    """The record's own identifiers, from its ArticleIdList and nowhere else.

    A blanket art.iter("ArticleId") also walks ReferenceList and CommentsCorrectionsList, so
    the DOI and PMCID that come back belong to the last work the article happens to cite.
    Restrict to the declared paths.
    """
    ids = {}
    for path in paths:
        for i in art.findall(path):
            t, v = i.get("IdType"), (i.text or "").strip()
            if t and v and t not in ids:
                ids[t] = v
    pmc = ids.get("pmc", "")
    if pmc and not pmc.upper().startswith("PMC"):
        ids["pmc"] = "PMC" + pmc
    return ids

## 1. Why this design exists

R01 found that meta-research articles about data sharing point at a repository of their own
about four times as often as meta-research articles on other subjects. That result is
exploratory and cannot be made confirmatory after the fact, for three reasons stated on its
own page: the grading rule was developed by reading the analysis data, three of four
sensitivity analyses were post hoc, and no human validation was done.

This specifies a confirmatory test of the same hypothesis, on a sample R01 did not use,
with every instrument fixed before any data are retrieved.

## 2. Objective and hypothesis

**Primary objective.** To test whether a meta-research article whose subject is data sharing,
code availability or research transparency is more likely than a meta-research article on
another aspect of research practice to carry an actionable data availability statement of its
own.

**Primary hypothesis.** It is, and the association is directional.

**Primary null.** The odds ratio for the transparency exposure is 1.

**What would falsify the hypothesis.** An adjusted odds ratio whose 95 percent confidence
interval includes 1, in a sample powered as in section 7. Section 7 fixes the power in
advance so that a null can be read as evidence of absence rather than absence of evidence,
which was not true of R01.

**Prior expectation, stated so it cannot be revised.** R01 estimated 4.40 (1.80 to 10.75) on
a different sample with a rule developed on that sample. Regression to the mean and the
removal of rule-development bias both predict a smaller effect here. An estimate between 1.5
and 3 would be consistent with R01; an estimate at or above 4 would be surprising and should
itself be treated with suspicion rather than as confirmation.

## 3. Design

Cross-sectional study of published articles. The unit of analysis is one article.

## 4. Sample

### 4.1 Frame

The frame is the qualified tier B search described in [methods](../methods.qmd), which ANDs a
topic block with a study-of-literature qualifier. Measured in PubMed on 2026-09-16:

| block | qualified hits | already in tier A | available to this study |
|---|---|---|---|
| `datashare` | 754 | 27 | **727** |
| `report` | 2392 | 103 | **2289** |
| `reg` | 4450 | 88 | **4362** |
| `repro` | 229 | 14 | **215** |

**Every record already present in the tier A corpus is excluded**, by PMID, before screening.
That exclusion is what makes this sample independent of R01, and it is applied first so that
no later decision can reintroduce an overlapping record.

### 4.2 Eligibility

**Include** an article when all of the following hold:

1. Retrieved by the qualified tier B search for one of the four blocks above.
2. Not present in the tier A corpus harvested on 2026-09-16.
3. Screened as meta-research: its object of study is published research, researchers, journals
   or the research process, rather than patients, organisms, materials or clinical outcomes.
4. Present in PMC with a `<body>` element longer than 500 characters, so that a statement
   could have been extracted had one existed.

**Exclude** an article when any of the following hold:

1. Its publication type is comment, editorial, letter, news, biography or historical article.
   R01 estimated an odds ratio of 0.04 for this group; an article with no data of its own
   belongs to a different population and its absence of a statement is not evidence about
   sharing behavior. R01 adjusted for this. This protocol excludes it, which is cleaner.
2. It is a retracted publication or a retraction notice.
3. It is not in English. R01 applied no language limit; a statement in another language would
   be graded `none` by an English-language rule, which is a measurement failure rather than a
   finding.

### 4.3 Screening

Criterion 3 is the one no boolean query settles, and the reason tier B was never harvested for
the map. Screening is on title and abstract, by two models from different providers, each given
the same written criterion and blind to the other.

- Both agree include: included.
- Both agree exclude: excluded.
- Disagreement: adjudicated by the author. The count of disagreements is reported.

**Before screening the frame**, 200 records sampled at random across the four blocks are
screened by the author to create a reference standard. Agreement is reported as Cohen's kappa
per model. **If neither model reaches kappa 0.75 against the reference standard, screening by
model is abandoned** and the study proceeds on a random subsample screened entirely by the
author, with the sample size reduced accordingly and the power recomputed and reported.

## 5. Exposure

**Exposed**: retrieved by the qualified `datashare` block and screened in.

**Unexposed**: retrieved by the qualified `report`, `reg` or `repro` blocks, screened in, and
not also retrieved by the `datashare` block.

An article retrieved by `datashare` and by another block is **exposed**. The exposure is
whether transparency is among the article's subjects, not whether it is the only one.

This is a coarser exposure than a judgment of what an article is primarily about, and that is
deliberate: it is reproducible from a query string, and its misclassification is toward the
null. The alternative, asking a model what each article is mainly about, adds a second
instrument needing its own validation to measure something the query already approximates.

## 6. Outcome

### 6.1 Extraction

The data availability statement is taken from the JATS full text by
`studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/extract.py` **as it stands at the commit this protocol is
published in**, unchanged. That code searches `<front>` and `<back>` only, never `<body>`, in
this order: a section or notes block with a `sec-type` or `notes-type` naming data
availability; a section or notes block whose title matches the data availability pattern; a
`custom-meta` pair whose name matches it.

The restriction to front and back matter is not a detail. Without it, a paper *about* data
availability has its own methods and results read as its disclosure, which is the artifact
that contaminated 4 of 43 exposed records in R01 and is documented there.

### 6.2 Grading

Three ordered levels, graded by the rule in `R/analyze.R` at the same commit, reproduced here
in full so that no later edit can pass unnoticed:

| grade | definition |
|---|---|
| `none` | no statement in front or back matter |
| `declarative` | a statement that shares nothing: available on request, contained within the article or its supplementary files, or not applicable |
| `actionable` | a named repository, or a DOI or URL that is not the article's own |

Tests apply in this order, and the order is itself pre-specified because changing it changes
the result:

1. A named repository present, from the fixed list in `REPO`: **actionable**. A repository
   outranks a co-occurring on-request clause, because an article that deposits its code and
   separately withholds licensed raw data has shared what it could.
2. An on-request clause present: **declarative**.
3. A not-applicable clause present: **declarative**.
4. A DOI or URL present that is not the article's own DOI: **actionable**.
5. Otherwise: **declarative**.

**Primary outcome**: `actionable` versus not.

**Secondary outcome**: the three-level ordinal grade.

### 6.3 Validation of the grading rule

**This is a gate, not a report.** 150 statements, stratified by rule grade, are graded by the
author, blind to the article's exposure and to the rule's grade. Agreement is reported as
Cohen's kappa with a 95 percent confidence interval.

- **Kappa at or above 0.80**: the rule is used as the primary instrument.
- **Kappa 0.60 to 0.79**: the rule is used, and the primary analysis is repeated on the
  hand-graded subsample alone as a co-primary, with both reported together regardless of
  whether they agree.
- **Kappa below 0.60**: the rule is abandoned. The study proceeds on a hand-graded random
  sample, with power recomputed and reported.

The rule is not modified after seeing the validation. If validation shows it is wrong, the
wrongness is reported and the fallback above is taken. This is the commitment R01 could not
make because its rule was written while the data were being read.

## 7. Sample size and power

**Not yet computed. This section is filled in by probes P1 to P5 in section 10 and this
document does not become a protocol until it is.**

What goes here when the probes have run: the realized exposed and unexposed counts after
exclusions, the observed actionable rate in the comparison arm, and the detectable odds ratio
at 80 percent power, each emitted by the probe code and asserted against this text rather than
retyped into it.

What is already known and did not need a probe: the frame sizes in section 4.1, measured by
`esearch` on 2026-09-16, and R01's comparison-arm actionable rate of 0.205, which is a
different sample and is a planning reference rather than an assumption to carry forward.

An earlier version of this section asserted "60 percent are in PMC with full text and a
screening yield of 60 percent gives roughly 260 exposed". Neither figure was measured. The
first is plausible because the tier A corpus ran at 59.5 percent; the second was invented.

## 8. Analysis

In R. All models fitted with `lme4` or `ordinal`.

**Primary.**

```r
glmer(actionable ~ exposed + year_c + (1 | journal),
      family = binomial, data = d,
      control = glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa"))
```

Article type does not enter the model because section 4.2 excludes the type that mattered.

**Secondary, preregistered.**

```r
clmm(grade ~ exposed + year_c + (1 | journal), data = d)
```

**Sensitivity analyses, all preregistered here, all reported whatever they show.**

1. Supplementary material graded actionable rather than declarative.
2. Publisher-typed extraction routes only, excluding title-matched routes.
3. Journal as a fixed effect, restricted to journals contributing at least 10 articles in each
   arm, as a within-journal estimate that does not rely on the random-effect assumption.
4. Excluding any article authored by the author of this project.
5. Excluding the `repro` block from the comparison arm, since reproducibility and data sharing
   are adjacent subjects and their separation is the weakest part of the exposure definition.

**Missing data.** Year is present for every PubMed record. Journal is present for every record.
No imputation is performed and no analysis drops a record for missingness; records are excluded
only by section 4.2.

**Multiplicity.** There is one primary comparison. The secondary and the five sensitivity
analyses are not corrected for multiplicity and are not used to claim significance; they exist
to show whether the primary estimate is fragile.

## 9. Threats, and what happened to each

A threat that is disclosed rather than removed is acceptable. A threat that is silently absent
is not. Every design choice made after seeing a number is listed, because R01 was exploratory
and anything carried forward from it makes a later choice suspect unless it is named.

| threat | what was done | removed or disclosed |
|---|---|---|
| Grading rule was developed on R01's data | Rule frozen at the pinned commit and applied unchanged to a sample it has never seen | Removed |
| Extractor read papers' own results as their disclosure | Restricted to `<front>` and `<back>`; the artifact and its cost are documented in R01 | Removed |
| Repository regex matched inside longer words | Word boundaries added before pinning | Removed |
| Exposure is a query, not a judgment of subject | Kept deliberately, for reproducibility; misclassification is toward the null | Disclosed |
| Comparison arm blocks chosen after seeing R01 | `report`, `reg` and `repro` were picked because they are the other large qualified blocks, not because of any result; sensitivity 5 drops `repro` as the most arguable | Disclosed |
| PMC coverage is not random | Not fixable in this design | Disclosed |
| Screening depends on a model | Gated on kappa against a human reference standard, with a stated fallback | Disclosed |
| Comparison-arm rate of 0.205 comes from R01 | Used for planning only; the realized rate is computed in probe P4 and is what the power calculation uses | Disclosed |

## 10. The probes that must run before this is a protocol

Each probe names the number it produces, the section that number goes into, what result would
change the design, and its cost. Nothing here is an argument; each one is a count.

| probe | computes | goes to | what would change the design | cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | Records retrieved by the four qualified blocks, after removing every tier A PMID | 4.1 | An available count far below 727 for `datashare` makes the confirmatory arm underpowered and the design moves to a prospective re-harvest instead | ~10 min, cached |
| **P2** | Proportion of P1 records carrying a PMCID | 4.2, 7 | Below about 40 percent and the realized exposed count falls under 150, at which point this is no better powered than R01 and is not worth running | minutes |
| **P3** | Proportion of P2 records with a `<body>` over 500 characters, and the extraction route distribution on this frame | 4.2, 6.1 | A route distribution unlike tier A's means publishers differ between frames and journal adjustment is carrying more weight than assumed | ~20 min, cached |
| **P4** | Grade distribution on the comparison arm under the pinned rule | 7 | An actionable rate far from R01's 0.205 changes every power figure | seconds, after P3 |
| **P5** | Detectable odds ratio at 80 percent power from the realized counts in P1 to P4 | 7 | A detectable effect above about 2.5 means the study cannot distinguish R01's estimate from a much smaller one and the frame must be widened | seconds |
| **P6** | Screening agreement: Cohen's kappa of each model against a 200-record human reference standard | 4.3 | Below 0.75 for every model triggers the hand-screening fallback and a smaller sample | 200 records to label |
| **P7** | Grading agreement: Cohen's kappa of the pinned rule against 150 human-graded statements | 6.3 | Below 0.60 abandons the rule, per 6.3 | 150 statements to label |

**P6 and P7 need a person, and that person is the author, not a model.** A model grading a
sample to validate a rule another model wrote is not a reference standard, and R01 already
shows what that is worth: the 40 statements graded by the model that wrote the rule found a
real defect and still left the rule's agreement with a human unknown. The two CSVs are
produced with empty label columns and handed over.

P6 may use a single model while the second reviewer is quota-blocked. Measuring an instrument
is not reviewing a study, so the two-reviewer rule does not apply to it; it applies to sections
under review, and it is not waived for those.

## 11. Deviations

Any departure from this protocol is recorded in a `DEVIATIONS` section appended to the result
page, stating what changed, when, why, and the estimate before and after where that can be
computed. A deviation is not a failure; concealing one is.

## 12. What this design still cannot do

- **It is not causal.** Researchers who study transparency and researchers who practice it may
  be the same people, and the design cannot separate selection from influence.
- **PMC coverage is not random.** Open-access availability correlates with transparency
  practice, so both arms lean transparent. The direction of bias on a *comparison* between arms
  is not obvious and is not claimed to be toward the null.
- **The qualified frame is lossy.** The study-of-literature qualifier cut the raw `datashare`
  block from 15418 to 754, and a meta-research paper describing its sample as "we examined 300
  trials" carries none of the qualifier phrases and is absent from the frame. The sample is a
  high-precision slice of the field, not the field.
- **It tests association in one direction only.** A finding that transparency-topic papers
  share more does not tell us whether writing about transparency changes behavior or whether
  people who already share choose to write about it.

published in. The analysis code that will run is already written and public; it is the code
that produced R01, and section 6 pins it.

## The author's own papers are in this corpus

A tier A search for meta-research retrieves the work of anyone who does meta-research,
including whoever is running the study. Two articles in the analysis set name the author's own
repositories. Both are in the exposed arm and both are graded actionable, so their presence
pushes the estimate in the direction of the hypothesis. Removing them changes the odds ratio
from 4.40 to 4.10. Reported rather than silently dropped.

## What this does not show

- **It is not causal and the design cannot make it so.** A researcher who studies data sharing
  and a researcher who shares data may simply be the same kind of person, or may work in the
  same few journals with the same few policies. The journal random intercept absorbs the
  average journal but not an interaction between a journal's policy and a paper's topic.
- **43 exposed records is thin.** The smallest effect this design could have detected at 80
  percent power was a rise from 20.5 to 48.9 percent. The observed effect is larger than that
  floor, which is why it is visible; an effect half this size would have been missed, and a
  null here would have ruled out very little.
- **The exposure is a vocabulary match, not a judgment.** A paper labelled as a transparency
  topic may mention data sharing once in passing. That misclassification is non-differential
  with respect to the outcome and biases toward the null, so it weakens this finding rather
  than manufacturing it.
- **60 percent PMC coverage is not random.** 742 corpus records have no PMCID and 89 more are
  in PMC as metadata only. Open-access availability is itself correlated with transparency
  practice, so the sample leans transparent. This is stated in the protocol and is not fixable
  within the design.
- **The grading rule has been validated by a model and not yet by a person.** See below.

## Validation still outstanding

The protocol calls for 100 statements graded by hand to measure where the rule disagrees.
`studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/out/validation-sample.csv` holds that sample with an empty
`hand_grade` column, and the agreement will be reported here when it is filled in.

What has happened instead is weaker and is labeled as such: 40 statements were read and graded
by the same model that wrote the rule, which found the second defect above. A model checking
its own rule is not an independent check, and the kappa against a person is not yet known.
