## 1. The question

Meta-research criticizes the literature, and peer reviewers are drawn from the literature being
criticized. If review systematically blunts that criticism, the published record understates
what the field found, and the preprint is the only place the original claim survives.

This is measurable because meta-research increasingly appears as a preprint first, and both
versions persist.

## 2. Hypothesis

The conclusion of a meta-research article is less critical of the practice it studied in the
published version than in the preprint.

**Primary null.** Conclusion direction does not change between versions.

**What would falsify the hypothesis.** No net shift, or a shift toward more criticism. Either
is publishable and the second would be the more interesting.

## 3. Design

Paired before-and-after comparison within article. Each article is its own control, which
removes the between-article confounding that every other design here has to manage.

## 4. Sample

Meta-research articles with both a preprint and a published version, identified from OpenAlex
`locations` where a source is a named preprint server.

|---|---|---|---|

| tier B frame | 7572 | 75 | 1.0 percent |

Servers, frame: `[['osf preprints (osf preprints)', 19], ['zenodo (cern european organization for nuclear research)', 17], ['medrxiv', 16], ['arxiv (cornell university)', 10], ['research square', 6], ['biorxiv (cold spring harbor laboratory)', 5], ['ssrn electronic journal', 3]]`

### The decision rule, written before either probe returned

- **80 to 200**: reduced protocol, single binary outcome, no subgroups.
- **Below 80**: agenda item, like [P04](P04-observational-about-itself.qmd).

## 5. Exposure and outcome

There is no exposure; the contrast is version. The outcome is the direction and strength of the
concluding claim, extracted from the abstract conclusion and the discussion of each version and
scored on a five-point scale from strongly critical to endorsing.

Scoring is by the same gated procedure as [P03](P03-bad-news-citation.qmd): the author labels a
reference sample, two models label it blind, and the study does not proceed unless a model
reaches kappa 0.70 against the author.

**The scorer must be blind to version.** The two texts are presented in random order with
dates, venue and version labels stripped, because a scorer who knows which text is the preprint
knows which direction the hypothesis predicts.

## 6. Analysis, in the reduced form

The five-point ordinal outcome is **not** used. At 141 pairs it would spread the sample across
five levels and two directions. The outcome is binary: did the concluding claim become less
critical, yes or no, with unchanged pairs retained as "no".

```r
# McNemar on the paired binary shift. No covariates, no subgroups: at this sample
# size every added term is a term the data cannot support.
mcnemar.test(table(d$preprint_critical, d$published_critical))
```

Reported alongside: the raw 2x2 table, an exact binomial test on the discordant pairs, and the
count of pairs by preprint server so a reader can see the arXiv concentration for themselves.

Had the probes returned 200 or more pairs, section 6 would have specified `clmm(score ~ version
+ (1 | article))` on the ordinal score. It did not, so it does not.

- Test name
- Steps to execute
- Expected result

# Debugging

You are a hypothesis-driven debugger. Two disciplines apply regardless of language, runtime, or whether you have source:

1. **Runtime truth beats code reading.** Every claim about why the bug happens must come from observed state — never from a plausible story spun from reading code.
2. **Leave no trace.** Debugging creates artifacts. Every artifact is journaled and removed before you call the task done.

The rest of this file is a map. **The knowledge is in `references/`.** This file cannot teach you how to debug — it can only tell you which reference will, for your exact situation.

## Non-Negotiable Safety Invariants

<safety>

## 1. The question

Meta-research criticizes the literature, and peer reviewers are drawn from the literature being
criticized. If review systematically blunts that criticism, the published record understates
what the field found, and the preprint is the only place the original claim survives.

This is measurable because meta-research increasingly appears as a preprint first, and both
versions persist.

## 2. Hypothesis

The conclusion of a meta-research article is less critical of the practice it studied in the
published version than in the preprint.

**Primary null.** Conclusion direction does not change between versions.

**What would falsify the hypothesis.** No net shift, or a shift toward more criticism. Either
is publishable and the second would be the more interesting.

## 3. Design

Paired before-and-after comparison within article. Each article is its own control, which
removes the between-article confounding that every other design here has to manage.

## 4. Sample

Meta-research articles with both a preprint and a published version, identified from OpenAlex
`locations` where a source is a named preprint server.

| frame | resolved in OpenAlex | with a preprint | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| tier A | 1790 | 66 | 3.7 percent |
| tier B frame | 7572 | 75 | 1.0 percent |
| **combined** | | **141** | |

Servers, tier A: `[['osf preprints (osf preprints)', 31], ['arxiv (cornell university)', 24], ['medrxiv', 4], ['research square', 3], ['biorxiv (cold spring harbor laboratory)', 3], ['metarxiv (osf preprints)', 2], ['psyarxiv (osf preprints)', 1], ['zenodo (cern european organization for nuclear research)', 1]]`

Servers, frame: `[['osf preprints (osf preprints)', 19], ['zenodo (cern european organization for nuclear research)', 17], ['medrxiv', 16], ['arxiv (cornell university)', 10], ['research square', 6], ['biorxiv (cold spring harbor laboratory)', 5], ['ssrn electronic journal', 3]]`

**arXiv and SSRN contribute 33 of the 141 pairs and are mostly science-of-science
rather than biomedical meta-research.** Excluding them leaves 108, still within the 80 to 200
band, so **the primary analysis is restricted to biomedical preprint servers** and the full
141 pairs are reported as a preregistered secondary. Neither is a subgroup fished for after
the fact: both counts were known before either analysis was specified, and both are published.

### The decision rule, written before either probe returned

- **200 or more pairs**: full protocol, five-point ordinal outcome, subgroup analyses.
- **80 to 200**: reduced protocol, single binary outcome, no subgroups.
- **Below 80**: agenda item, like [P04](P04-observational-about-itself.qmd).

**Observed: 141. The reduced form applies.**

## 5. Exposure and outcome

There is no exposure; the contrast is version. The outcome is the direction and strength of the
concluding claim, extracted from the abstract conclusion and the discussion of each version and
scored on a five-point scale from strongly critical to endorsing.

Scoring is by the same gated procedure as [P03](P03-bad-news-citation.qmd): the author labels a
reference sample, two models label it blind, and the study does not proceed unless a model
reaches kappa 0.70 against the author.

**The scorer must be blind to version.** The two texts are presented in random order with
dates, venue and version labels stripped, because a scorer who knows which text is the preprint
knows which direction the hypothesis predicts.

## 6. Analysis, in the reduced form

The five-point ordinal outcome is **not** used. At 141 pairs it would spread the sample across
five levels and two directions. The outcome is binary: did the concluding claim become less
critical, yes or no, with unchanged pairs retained as "no".

```r
# McNemar on the paired binary shift. No covariates, no subgroups: at this sample
# size every added term is a term the data cannot support.
mcnemar.test(table(d$preprint_critical, d$published_critical))
```

Reported alongside: the raw 2x2 table, an exact binomial test on the discordant pairs, and the
count of pairs by preprint server so a reader can see the arXiv concentration for themselves.

Had the probes returned 200 or more pairs, section 6 would have specified `clmm(score ~ version
+ (1 | article))` on the ordinal score. It did not, so it does not.

| Between-article confounding | Paired design; each article is its own control | Removed |

| Peer review is not the only difference | Authors revise for many reasons; the design attributes nothing to review specifically | Disclosed |

## Status: protocol. Citation data retrieved. The classifier gate is outstanding. Not registered.

The citation probe ran on 2026-09-16: 1790 of 1805
tier A PMIDs resolved in OpenAlex, a match rate of 99.2 percent.

**The exposure has not been measured and cannot be until 300 abstracts are classified by the
author.** Section 6 carries a stopping rule written before any classification has been run.

Awaiting review by two models; the second is quota-blocked until 2026-09-20.

## 1. The question

Meta-research reports on how well other research is done, and the answer is usually that it is
done badly. If findings of failure are rewarded with citations more than findings that a
practice is adequate, the field faces an incentive gradient pointing at one conclusion, and
that gradient would be invisible within any single study.

This is publication bias asked about the field that studies publication bias, and asked at the
citation stage rather than at acceptance.

## 2. Hypothesis

Among meta-research articles, those concluding that the practice studied is inadequate accrue
more citations than those concluding it is adequate, adjusted for age, journal and topic.

**Primary null.** The incidence rate ratio for conclusion direction is 1.

**What would falsify it.** An interval containing 1.

## 3. Design

Cross-sectional study of published articles with a count outcome.

## 4. Sample

The tier A corpus, all 1805 records carrying a PMID.
1790 resolve in OpenAlex.

Articles from 2025 and 2026 are retained and age is modeled, not trimmed; excluding recent work
would drop 76 percent of the corpus.

## 5. Outcome

`cited_by_count` from OpenAlex, retrieved on 2026-09-16 and frozen at
`data/lit/openalex-records.jsonl`. The date matters: citation counts move, and an analysis run
against a live API is not reproducible.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| median citations | 8 |
| mean citations | 36.29 |
| 90th percentile | 75 |
| maximum | 2937 |
| articles with zero citations | 284 |

The distribution is heavily skewed, mean 36.29 against median
8, which is why the model is negative binomial and why a quantile
regression on the upper deciles is fitted alongside. The incentive argument lives in the tail
and a mean effect would not find it.

284 articles have zero citations. They are retained; a zero is informative
about a paper nobody used and dropping them would condition on the outcome.

## 6. Exposure, and the gate

The exposure is the direction of the article's own conclusion, and no rule extracts it from an
abstract. This is the one study of the set whose exposure needs a model to read text, and that
is a liability rather than a convenience.

Classification is therefore treated as an instrument that must be validated before it is used:

1. A stratified random sample of **300 abstracts is classified by the author** into
   inadequate, adequate, mixed, or not applicable. This is the reference standard. A model
   does not produce it, for the reason R01 already demonstrated: a model checking a rule
   another model wrote found a real defect and still left agreement with a person unknown.
2. Two models from different providers classify the same 300, blind to the author's labels and
   to each other.
3. Agreement is reported as Cohen's kappa against the reference standard and between models.
4. **If neither model reaches kappa 0.70 against the author's labels, the study stops and the
   negative methodological result is published.** Whether a language model can read the
   direction of a scientific conclusion is itself worth knowing.
5. Only above the threshold does classification extend to the rest, with the measured
   misclassification rate carried into a sensitivity analysis.

The threshold is written here, before any classification has been run. A threshold chosen after
seeing the agreement is not a threshold.

## 7. Analysis

In R.

```r
glm.nb(cited_by_count ~ direction + poly(years_since_pub, 2) + topic_block + journal_size,
       data = d)
```

Age enters as a quadratic because citation accrual is not linear in time and the corpus spans
1965 to 2026. Preregistered alongside: quantile regression at the 75th and 90th percentiles,
and the same model with `direction` as measured by each classifier separately, so that a result
that depends on which model read the abstracts is visible as such.

## 8. Threats, and what happened to each

| threat | what was done | removed or disclosed |
|---|---|---|
| Reverse causation | Not addressable in this design. A widely cited article may attract a negative framing rather than the other way round | Disclosed |
| Topic confounds direction | Some topics almost never produce an adequate verdict, so adjusting for topic may absorb the effect. Reported adjusted and unadjusted, with the difference discussed rather than resolved | Disclosed |
| Classifier error | Gated on kappa against the author's labels, with a stated stop | Disclosed |
| Citation counts move | Frozen at a dated snapshot in the repository | Removed |
| Self-citation and field size | Not in the primary model; OpenAlex exposes the data and it is a stated secondary | Disclosed |
| Zero-citation articles | Retained; dropping them conditions on the outcome | Removed |

## 9. Probes

| probe | computes | goes to | what would change the design | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PA** | OpenAlex match rate and citation distribution | 4, 5 | a match rate below about 90 percent makes the sample unrepresentative | done |
| **PB** | classifier agreement against 300 author labels | 6 | below kappa 0.70 stops the study | outstanding, needs the author |
| **PC** | detectable rate ratio from the realized exposure split | 7 | computed once PB returns, because it depends on how many articles fall in each direction | outstanding, depends on PB |

## 10. Deviations

Recorded on the result page: what changed, when, why, and the estimate before and after.

## 11. What this cannot show

- It cannot show that negative findings are rewarded, only that they are cited more.
- It cannot separate the effect of the finding from the effect of the topic that produced it.
- OpenAlex citation counts include self-citations and citations from any source it indexes,
  which is broader than PubMed and not identical to it.

## The corpus

1830 unique PubMed and PMC records that name themselves meta-research, metascience, research on
research, or meta-epidemiological, harvested 2026-09-16. 1716 carry an abstract and 1315 carry
MeSH indexing. Every query, every count, and the two queries that turned out to be wrong are

Topic blocks by subject matter, such as data sharing and retraction, have been sized but not

## Building

```bash
python3 build/lit/search.py --check     # assert the parser still works, 5 records
python3 build/lit/search.py --counts    # size every block, download nothing
python3 build/lit/search.py --tier A    # harvest, resumable, cached

python3 build/render_site.py            # write landscape.qmd and corpus.qmd
quarto render                           # -> docs/
quarto preview                          # live preview
```

Rendering needs Quarto 1.8 or later. The harvest needs Python 3 and nothing else; it uses only
the standard library and no NCBI API key. Analyses in proposed studies are written in R.

`data/lit/records.jsonl` is tracked, so everything after the harvest rebuilds on a fresh clone
without touching NCBI. Pushing to `main` regenerates the derived pages, fails if they no longer
follow from the tracked corpus, then renders and publishes to `gh-pages`.

## The corpus

1830 unique PubMed and PMC records that name themselves meta-research, metascience, research on
research, or meta-epidemiological, harvested 2026-09-16. 1716 carry an abstract and 1315 carry
MeSH indexing. Every query, every count, and the two queries that turned out to be wrong are
recorded in `methods.qmd`.

Topic blocks by subject matter, such as data sharing and retraction, have been sized but not
harvested; a subject query retrieves studies that performed a practice alongside studies of it,
and separating those is a classification problem rather than a query to tune.

## Building

```bash
python3 build/lit/search.py --check     # assert the parser still works, 5 records
python3 build/lit/search.py --counts    # size every block, download nothing
python3 build/lit/search.py --tier A    # harvest, resumable, cached
python3 build/lit/describe.py           # derive map.json, MAP.md, records-labelled.jsonl
python3 build/render_site.py            # write landscape.qmd and corpus.qmd
quarto render                           # -> docs/
quarto preview                          # live preview
```

Rendering needs Quarto 1.8 or later. The harvest needs Python 3 and nothing else; it uses only
the standard library and no NCBI API key. Analyses in proposed studies are written in R.

`data/lit/records.jsonl` is tracked, so everything after the harvest rebuilds on a fresh clone
without touching NCBI. Pushing to `main` regenerates the derived pages, fails if they no longer
follow from the tracked corpus, then renders and publishes to `gh-pages`.

## Layout

| Path | What it is |
|---|---|
| `build/lit/search.py` | PubMed and PMC harvester. Owns the search blocks and the phrase set |
| `build/lit/describe.py` | Corpus to map. Vocabulary labelling, counts, co-occurrence |
| `build/render_site.py` | Map to Quarto source. Writes `landscape.qmd` and `corpus.qmd` |
| `data/lit/records.jsonl` | The corpus, tracked |
| `data/lit/search-log.json` | Every query issued, with hit counts and any truncation |
| `data/lit/counts.json` | Block sizes measured before harvesting |
| `methods.qmd` | The published search protocol, including what went wrong |

## Provenance

The searching, the analysis code, the drafting and the reviewing are done by language models.
The research questions, the scope decisions and the responsibility for everything published are
the author's. Where a model produced an artifact, the page says which model and when.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""OpenAlex probes shared by P03 and P06.

P03 needs citation counts on the tier A corpus. P06 needs to know how many of those records
have a preprint version. Both answers come from the same records, so both are fetched in one
pass of about 37 calls rather than twice.

Output: data/lit/openalex.jsonl, one line per matched record, and a printed summary.

Usage: python3 studies/_shared/openalex_probe.py
"""

import collections
import json
import os
import sys
import time
import urllib.parse
import urllib.request

HERE = os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__))
ROOT = os.path.dirname(os.path.dirname(HERE))
# Which corpus to resolve. Passing a stem rather than copying this file: a sed-edited copy
# in a temp directory resolved ROOT from its own location and looked for the corpus in the
# temp tree, which is the obvious failure and took a run to notice.
STEM = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else "records"
CORPUS = os.path.join(ROOT, "data", "lit", f"{STEM}.jsonl")
OUT = os.path.join(ROOT, "data", "lit", f"openalex-{STEM}.jsonl")
SUMMARY = os.path.join(ROOT, "data", "lit", f"openalex-{STEM}-summary.json")
MAILTO = "ahmad.pub@gmail.com"
UA = f"autometaresearch/1.0 (mailto:{MAILTO})"

# OpenAlex asks for a mailto and gives the polite pool in return. 50 ids per filter is the
# documented ceiling for an OR list.
BATCH = 50
SELECT = ("id,doi,ids,publication_year,cited_by_count,type,"
          "primary_location,locations,open_access,authorships,referenced_works_count")


def get(url, tries=4):
    for attempt in range(tries):
        try:
            req = urllib.request.Request(url, headers={"User-Agent": UA})
            with urllib.request.urlopen(req, timeout=90) as r:
                return json.loads(r.read().decode("utf8"))
        except Exception as e:  # noqa: BLE001
            if attempt == tries - 1:
                raise
            print(f"    retry {attempt + 1} after {e}", file=sys.stderr)
            time.sleep(2 ** attempt)
    return {}


def main():
    records = [json.loads(l) for l in open(CORPUS, encoding="utf8")]
    pmids = [r["pmid"] for r in records if r["pmid"]]
    print(f"{len(pmids)} PMIDs to resolve", file=sys.stderr)

    out = []
    for i in range(0, len(pmids), BATCH):
        batch = pmids[i:i + BATCH]
        q = urllib.parse.urlencode({
            "filter": "pmid:" + "|".join(batch),
            "select": SELECT, "per-page": 100, "mailto": MAILTO,
        })
        d = get(f"https://api.openalex.org/works?{q}")
        out.extend(d.get("results", []))
        print(f"  {min(i + BATCH, len(pmids))}/{len(pmids)}", end="\r", file=sys.stderr)
        time.sleep(0.15)
    print(file=sys.stderr)

    with open(OUT, "w", encoding="utf8") as fh:
        for w in out:
            fh.write(json.dumps(w, ensure_ascii=False) + "\n")

    cites = sorted(w.get("cited_by_count", 0) for w in out)
    # A preprint version means a location whose source is a repository, other than the
    # primary. PMC is a repository too, so a bare repository count would say "almost all of
    # them" and mean nothing; the preprint servers are named explicitly.
    PREPRINT = ("biorxiv", "medrxiv", "arxiv", "ssrn", "osf", "preprints.org",
                "research square", "zenodo", "psyarxiv", "metaarxiv")
    with_preprint, by_server = 0, collections.Counter()
    for w in out:
