Confirmatory protocol for P01 on an independent sample. Feasibility probes computed; two validation gates need human labels; awaiting peer review.
Published

September 16, 2026

ImportantStatus: BLOCKED BY REVIEW on 2026-09-19. Do not run this.

Both reviewers returned unsound. Three fatal findings and nineteen major, every severe one carrying a verbatim quote. The full review is at reviews/P01v2 and the verdict is blocked, which under the adjudication rule means an evidenced fatal finding stands unrebutted.

The two fatal findings were verified against primary source before publication, and both are correct.

  1. This is not a confirmatory protocol and calling it one was wrong. Probe P4 tabulated the primary outcome by arm on the confirmatory sample: 95 of 520 exposed against 132 of 3435 comparison, a crude odds ratio near 5.6. The hypothesis has already been looked at on this data. Running the planned model now would be confirming something already seen. Both reviewers found this independently, and it is the same class of error R01 was reclassified for, made again one document later while explicitly trying to avoid it.

  2. The outcome instrument has false negatives in both directions. PMC5808091 and PMC6092896 both carry a data availability statement and the pinned extractor returns nothing for them, because their statement is in <body> and extraction was restricted to <front> and <back>. That restriction was added to fix the contamination artifact documented in R01, and the cost of the fix was never measured. Separately, _text() keeps an ext-link’s visible text and discards its xlink:href, so PMC10202448’s statement reads “on the associated GitHub repository (” with the URL gone, and a real deposit grades declarative.

The document below is left exactly as reviewed. It is the record of what was submitted, not a corrected version, and a successor protocol will be a new document.

This supersedes P01, a one-page proposal left unedited as the record of what was actually pre-specified for R01.

1. Why this design exists

R01 found that meta-research articles about data sharing point at a repository of their own about four times as often as meta-research articles on other subjects. That result is exploratory and cannot be made confirmatory after the fact, for three reasons stated on its own page: the grading rule was developed by reading the analysis data, three of four sensitivity analyses were post hoc, and no human validation was done.

This specifies a confirmatory test of the same hypothesis, on a sample R01 did not use, with every instrument fixed before any data are retrieved.

2. Objective and hypothesis

Primary objective. To test whether a meta-research article whose subject is data sharing, code availability or research transparency is more likely than a meta-research article on another aspect of research practice to carry an actionable data availability statement of its own.

Primary hypothesis. It is, and the association is directional.

Primary null. The odds ratio for the transparency exposure is 1.

What would falsify the hypothesis. An adjusted odds ratio whose 95 percent confidence interval includes 1, in a sample powered as in section 7. Section 7 fixes the power in advance so that a null can be read as evidence of absence rather than absence of evidence, which was not true of R01.

Prior expectation, stated so it cannot be revised. R01 estimated 4.40 (1.80 to 10.75) on a different sample with a rule developed on that sample. Regression to the mean and the removal of rule-development bias both predict a smaller effect here. An estimate between 1.5 and 3 would be consistent with R01; an estimate at or above 4 would be surprising and should itself be treated with suspicion rather than as confirmation.

3. Design

Cross-sectional study of published articles. The unit of analysis is one article.

4. Sample

4.1 Frame

The frame is the qualified tier B search described in methods, which ANDs a topic block with a study-of-literature qualifier. Measured in PubMed on 2026-09-16:

block qualified hits already in tier A available to this study
datashare 754 27 727
report 2392 103 2289
reg 4450 88 4362
repro 229 14 215

Every record already present in the tier A corpus is excluded, by PMID, before screening. That exclusion is what makes this sample independent of R01, and it is applied first so that no later decision can reintroduce an overlapping record.

4.2 Eligibility

Include an article when all of the following hold:

  1. Retrieved by the qualified tier B search for one of the four blocks above.
  2. Not present in the tier A corpus harvested on 2026-09-16.
  3. Screened as meta-research: its object of study is published research, researchers, journals or the research process, rather than patients, organisms, materials or clinical outcomes.
  4. Present in PMC with a <body> element longer than 500 characters, so that a statement could have been extracted had one existed.

Exclude an article when any of the following hold:

  1. Its publication type is comment, editorial, letter, news, biography or historical article. R01 estimated an odds ratio of 0.04 for this group; an article with no data of its own belongs to a different population and its absence of a statement is not evidence about sharing behavior. R01 adjusted for this. This protocol excludes it, which is cleaner.
  2. It is a retracted publication or a retraction notice.
  3. It is not in English. R01 applied no language limit; a statement in another language would be graded none by an English-language rule, which is a measurement failure rather than a finding.

4.3 Screening

Criterion 3 is the one no boolean query settles, and the reason tier B was never harvested for the map. Screening is on title and abstract, by two models from different providers, each given the same written criterion and blind to the other.

  • Both agree include: included.
  • Both agree exclude: excluded.
  • Disagreement: adjudicated by the author. The count of disagreements is reported.

Before screening the frame, 200 records sampled at random across the four blocks are screened by the author to create a reference standard. Agreement is reported as Cohen’s kappa per model. If neither model reaches kappa 0.75 against the reference standard, screening by model is abandoned and the study proceeds on a random subsample screened entirely by the author, with the sample size reduced accordingly and the power recomputed and reported.

5. Exposure

Exposed: retrieved by the qualified datashare block and screened in.

Unexposed: retrieved by the qualified report, reg or repro blocks, screened in, and not also retrieved by the datashare block.

An article retrieved by datashare and by another block is exposed. The exposure is whether transparency is among the article’s subjects, not whether it is the only one.

This is a coarser exposure than a judgment of what an article is primarily about, and that is deliberate: it is reproducible from a query string, and its misclassification is toward the null. The alternative, asking a model what each article is mainly about, adds a second instrument needing its own validation to measure something the query already approximates.

6. Outcome

6.1 Extraction

The data availability statement is taken from the JATS full text by studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/extract.py as it stands at commit 158164be700a596610a074533e6509ce142a6774, unchanged. A prose pin is not a pin; that hash is the pin, and git show 158164be700a596610a074533e6509ce142a6774:studies/P01-advocacy-and-practice/extract.py is how a reader checks it. That code searches <front> and <back> only, never <body>, in this order: a section or notes block with a sec-type or notes-type naming data availability; a section or notes block whose title matches the data availability pattern; a custom-meta pair whose name matches it.

The restriction to front and back matter is not a detail. Without it, a paper about data availability has its own methods and results read as its disclosure, which is the artifact that contaminated 4 of 43 exposed records in R01 and is documented there.

6.2 Grading

Three ordered levels, graded by the rule in studies/_shared/R/grade.R at that same commit, reproduced here in full so that no later edit can pass unnoticed:

grade definition
none no statement in front or back matter
declarative a statement that shares nothing: available on request, contained within the article or its supplementary files, or not applicable
actionable a named repository, or a DOI or URL that is not the article’s own

Tests apply in this order, and the order is itself pre-specified because changing it changes the result:

  1. A named repository present, from the fixed list in REPO: actionable. A repository outranks a co-occurring on-request clause, because an article that deposits its code and separately withholds licensed raw data has shared what it could.
  2. An on-request clause present: declarative.
  3. A not-applicable clause present: declarative.
  4. A DOI or URL present that is not the article’s own DOI: actionable.
  5. Otherwise: declarative.

Primary outcome: actionable versus not.

Secondary outcome: the three-level ordinal grade.

6.3 Validation of the grading rule

This is a gate, not a report. 150 statements, stratified by rule grade, are graded by the author, blind to the article’s exposure and to the rule’s grade. Agreement is reported as Cohen’s kappa with a 95 percent confidence interval.

  • Kappa at or above 0.80: the rule is used as the primary instrument.
  • Kappa 0.60 to 0.79: the rule is used, and the primary analysis is repeated on the hand-graded subsample alone as a co-primary, with both reported together regardless of whether they agree.
  • Kappa below 0.60: the rule is abandoned. The study proceeds on a hand-graded random sample, with power recomputed and reported.

The rule is not modified after seeing the validation. If validation shows it is wrong, the wrongness is reported and the fallback above is taken. This is the commitment R01 could not make because its rule was written while the data were being read.

7. Sample size and power

Computed by probes P1 to P5 on 2026-09-16. Every number in this section is emitted by studies/P01v2-confirmatory/out/probes.json and is not retyped from anywhere.

Realized counts

step exposed comparison
frame, after removing 205 tier A overlaps from 7617 records 704 6526
carrying a PMCID 547 3770
with PMC full text, the analysis set before screening 520 3435

The exposed arm is 520, not the 260 the discarded assumption produced. The design is better powered than it was written to be.

The probe that changed the design

PMC coverage differs sharply between arms: 77.7 percent of exposed records against 57.8 percent of comparison records. The discarded assumption was a flat 60 percent for both, close to tier A’s 59.5 percent, and it would have concealed a 20 point differential.

This is not a nuisance; it is adjacent to the hypothesis. Papers about data sharing are much likelier to be openly available, which is itself a transparency behavior. The consequence for this study is that the two arms are selected differently into the analysis set, and section 9 carries it as a disclosed threat that the design cannot remove.

The baseline rate, and a reasoning error the probe caught

On the unscreened frame the comparison arm is 3.8 percent actionable and the exposed arm 18.3 percent.

A comment in the probe code asserted that the unscreened comparison rate would be an upper bound on the screened one, because primary studies carry journal-mandated statements. That was backwards. R01’s rate among screened meta-research was 20.5 percent against 3.8 percent here, so screening removes records that deposit less often and raises the comparison rate. The unscreened figure is a lower bound.

The power calculation therefore runs across a range of plausible screened baselines rather than at any single assumed one.

Detectable odds ratio at 80 percent power, alpha 0.05

Rows are the screened comparison-arm actionable rate; columns are the proportion of records surviving screening, which shrinks both arms.

baseline rate yield 0.5 yield 0.7 yield 1.0
0.038 2.18 1.97 1.79
0.1 1.72 1.6 1.49
0.15 1.6 1.5 1.41
0.205 1.53 1.44 1.36
0.25 1.5 1.41 1.34

Across the whole grid the detectable odds ratio is at most 2.18. R01’s point estimate was 4.40 and section 2 states that anything between 1.5 and 3 would be consistent with it. The design detects effects across that entire band, which is the property R01 lacked: its floor was roughly 3.7, so it could only ever have seen an effect as large as the one it reported.

A null result here rules something out.

8. Analysis

In R. All models fitted with lme4 or ordinal.

Primary.

glmer(actionable ~ exposed + year_c + (1 | journal),
      family = binomial, data = d,
      control = glmerControl(optimizer = "bobyqa"))

Article type does not enter the model because section 4.2 excludes the type that mattered.

Secondary, preregistered.

clmm(grade ~ exposed + year_c + (1 | journal), data = d)

Sensitivity analyses, all preregistered here, all reported whatever they show.

  1. Supplementary material graded actionable rather than declarative.
  2. Publisher-typed extraction routes only, excluding title-matched routes.
  3. Journal as a fixed effect, restricted to journals contributing at least 10 articles in each arm, as a within-journal estimate that does not rely on the random-effect assumption.
  4. Excluding any article authored by the author of this project.
  5. Excluding the repro block from the comparison arm, since reproducibility and data sharing are adjacent subjects and their separation is the weakest part of the exposure definition.
  6. Restricted to fully open-access journals, added after probe P2 found that PMC coverage differs by 20 points between arms. Within a fully open-access journal every article is in PMC, so the differential selection that P2 exposed cannot operate. This is a post-probe addition and is labeled as one.

Missing data. Year is present for every PubMed record. Journal is present for every record. No imputation is performed and no analysis drops a record for missingness; records are excluded only by section 4.2.

Multiplicity. There is one primary comparison. The secondary and the five sensitivity analyses are not corrected for multiplicity and are not used to claim significance; they exist to show whether the primary estimate is fragile.

9. Threats, and what happened to each

A threat that is disclosed rather than removed is acceptable. A threat that is silently absent is not. Every design choice made after seeing a number is listed, because R01 was exploratory and anything carried forward from it makes a later choice suspect unless it is named.

threat what was done removed or disclosed
Grading rule was developed on R01’s data Rule frozen at the pinned commit and applied unchanged to a sample it has never seen Removed
Extractor read papers’ own results as their disclosure Restricted to <front> and <back>; the artifact and its cost are documented in R01 Removed
Repository regex matched inside longer words Word boundaries added before pinning Removed
Exposure is a query, not a judgment of subject Kept deliberately, for reproducibility; misclassification is toward the null Disclosed
Comparison arm blocks chosen after seeing R01 report, reg and repro were picked because they are the other large qualified blocks, not because of any result; sensitivity 5 drops repro as the most arguable Disclosed
PMC coverage is not random Not fixable in this design Disclosed
PMC coverage differs by arm, 77.7 percent exposed against 57.8 percent comparison Found by probe P2, not anticipated when the design was written. Arms are differentially selected into the analysis set and the direction favors the hypothesis. Reported as a primary limitation; a sensitivity restricted to journals that are fully open access is added as analysis 6 Disclosed, not removed
Screening depends on a model Gated on kappa against a human reference standard, with a stated fallback Disclosed
Comparison-arm rate of 0.205 comes from R01 Used for planning only; the realized rate is computed in probe P4 and is what the power calculation uses Disclosed

10. The probes that must run before this is a protocol

Each probe names the number it produces, the section that number goes into, what result would change the design, and its cost. Nothing here is an argument; each one is a count.

probe computes goes to what would change the design cost
P1 done Records retrieved by the four qualified blocks, after removing every tier A PMID 4.1 An available count far below 727 for datashare makes the confirmatory arm underpowered and the design moves to a prospective re-harvest instead ~10 min, cached
P2 done Proportion of P1 records carrying a PMCID 4.2, 7 Below about 40 percent and the realized exposed count falls under 150, at which point this is no better powered than R01 and is not worth running minutes
P3 done Proportion of P2 records with a <body> over 500 characters, and the extraction route distribution on this frame 4.2, 6.1 A route distribution unlike tier A’s means publishers differ between frames and journal adjustment is carrying more weight than assumed ~20 min, cached
P4 done Grade distribution on the comparison arm under the pinned rule 7 An actionable rate far from R01’s 0.205 changes every power figure seconds, after P3
P5 done Detectable odds ratio at 80 percent power from the realized counts in P1 to P4 7 A detectable effect above about 2.5 means the study cannot distinguish R01’s estimate from a much smaller one and the frame must be widened seconds
P6 outstanding, needs the author Screening agreement: Cohen’s kappa of each model against a 200-record human reference standard 4.3 Below 0.75 for every model triggers the hand-screening fallback and a smaller sample 200 records to label
P7 outstanding, needs the author Grading agreement: Cohen’s kappa of the pinned rule against 150 human-graded statements 6.3 Below 0.60 abandons the rule, per 6.3 150 statements to label

P6 and P7 need a person, and that person is the author, not a model. A model grading a sample to validate a rule another model wrote is not a reference standard, and R01 already shows what that is worth: the 40 statements graded by the model that wrote the rule found a real defect and still left the rule’s agreement with a human unknown. The two CSVs are produced with empty label columns and handed over.

P6 may use a single model while the second reviewer is quota-blocked. Measuring an instrument is not reviewing a study, so the two-reviewer rule does not apply to it; it applies to sections under review, and it is not waived for those.

11. Deviations

Any departure from this protocol is recorded in a DEVIATIONS section appended to the result page, stating what changed, when, why, and the estimate before and after where that can be computed. A deviation is not a failure; concealing one is.

12. What this design still cannot do

  • It is not causal. Researchers who study transparency and researchers who practice it may be the same people, and the design cannot separate selection from influence.
  • PMC coverage is not random. Open-access availability correlates with transparency practice, so both arms lean transparent. The direction of bias on a comparison between arms is not obvious and is not claimed to be toward the null.
  • The qualified frame is lossy. The study-of-literature qualifier cut the raw datashare block from 15418 to 754, and a meta-research paper describing its sample as “we examined 300 trials” carries none of the qualifier phrases and is absent from the frame. The sample is a high-precision slice of the field, not the field.
  • It tests association in one direction only. A finding that transparency-topic papers share more does not tell us whether writing about transparency changes behavior or whether people who already share choose to write about it.
