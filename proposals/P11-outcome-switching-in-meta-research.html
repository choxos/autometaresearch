Does meta-research switch its own outcomes?

design
reg
reflexive
Whether meta-research results papers depart from their published protocols less often than trials in the same journals.
Published

September 22, 2026

ImportantStatus: design. Depends on P10’s linkage. No probe has run.

Written against the design standard.

1. The question

Outcome switching is one of meta-research’s best known findings about trials: the primary outcome in the paper is not the one in the protocol, and the paper does not say so. Meta-research publishes protocols too. Whether it keeps to them has not been asked.

2. Prior work, and the search behind the novelty claim

Log key P11.

corpus regex: (meta-research|methodological (review|stud)|meta-epidemiolog).{0,80}
              (discrepanc|switch|deviat).{0,40}protocol
PubMed:       ("outcome switching"[tiab] OR "discrepancies between protocol"[tiab]
              OR "protocol deviations"[tiab]) AND ("meta-research"[tiab]
              OR "methodological studies"[tiab] OR "meta-epidemiological"[tiab]
              OR "research on research"[tiab])

Six PubMed records, one corpus record. The search finds outcome switching studied in cohort studies of interventions (PMID 42203255) and in follow-up publications of randomized trials (PMID 34004339), and a corpus study of repeated changes to prespecified primary outcomes in trials (PMID 37906614). None examines meta-research’s own protocols.

3. Hypothesis

A meta-research results paper contains an undisclosed discrepancy from its protocol’s primary outcome less often than a trial results paper from the same journal and year.

Null. The odds ratio for an undisclosed primary-outcome discrepancy, meta-research against trials, is 1.

What would falsify it. An odds ratio at or above 1. The field that named the problem may be expected to avoid it; finding otherwise is the more consequential result.

4. Design and unit

Cross-sectional comparison of linked protocol and results pairs. One pair is one unit.

5. Sample

Every pair P10 links, in both arms, restricted to pairs where the protocol states a primary outcome. Protocols that name no primary outcome are counted and reported, since not naming one is itself a finding, but cannot enter the primary comparison.

6. Exposure

Pair type: meta-research or trial, inherited from P10.

7. Outcome and instrument

A discrepancy is any of: primary outcome added, omitted, replaced, demoted to secondary, or redefined (different measure, timepoint or unit of analysis). It is undisclosed when the results paper does not state the change. For meta-research, a primary outcome is the principal estimand the protocol names, for example the proportion of trials sharing data, not a clinical endpoint; the rubric gives worked examples of both.

Each pair is coded by a model and by the author, blind to each other. The rubric is published with the protocol before coding starts (S8).

8. Validation gate

Agreement is computed on the contrast that enters the model: undisclosed discrepancy, yes or no. Below kappa 0.70 between model and author on the first 60 pairs, the model is dropped and the author codes every pair. The pair count may make that feasible; probe PA says whether it does.

9. Analysis

In R.

glmer(undisclosed ~ type + (1 | journal), family = binomial, data = d)

Preregistered alongside: the same model for any discrepancy, disclosed or not, and a breakdown by discrepancy type, reported descriptively without significance tests.

12. What this cannot show

  • Whether a switch changed the conclusion.
  • Why a switch happened. A defensible change and a convenient one look the same in the text unless the paper explains itself, which is what “undisclosed” measures.
