Is bad news cited more?

protocol
biblio
incentives
Whether meta-research reporting poor practice accrues more citations than meta-research reporting good practice. Citation probe computed; the classifier is the gate.
Published

September 16, 2026

ImportantStatus: protocol. Citation data retrieved. The classifier gate is outstanding. Not registered.

The citation probe ran on 2026-09-16: 1790 of 1805 tier A PMIDs resolved in OpenAlex, a match rate of 99.2 percent.

The exposure has not been measured and cannot be until 300 abstracts are classified by the author. Section 6 carries a stopping rule written before any classification has been run.

Awaiting review by two models; the second is quota-blocked until 2026-09-20.

1. The question

Meta-research reports on how well other research is done, and the answer is usually that it is done badly. If findings of failure are rewarded with citations more than findings that a practice is adequate, the field faces an incentive gradient pointing at one conclusion, and that gradient would be invisible within any single study.

This is publication bias asked about the field that studies publication bias, and asked at the citation stage rather than at acceptance.

2. Hypothesis

Among meta-research articles, those concluding that the practice studied is inadequate accrue more citations than those concluding it is adequate, adjusted for age, journal and topic.

Primary null. The incidence rate ratio for conclusion direction is 1.

What would falsify it. An interval containing 1.

3. Design

Cross-sectional study of published articles with a count outcome.

4. Sample

The tier A corpus, all 1805 records carrying a PMID. 1790 resolve in OpenAlex.

Articles from 2025 and 2026 are retained and age is modeled, not trimmed; excluding recent work would drop 76 percent of the corpus.

5. Outcome

cited_by_count from OpenAlex, retrieved on 2026-09-16 and frozen at data/lit/openalex-records.jsonl. The date matters: citation counts move, and an analysis run against a live API is not reproducible.

quantity value

The distribution is heavily skewed, mean 36.29 against median 8, which is why the model is negative binomial and why a quantile regression on the upper deciles is fitted alongside. The incentive argument lives in the tail and a mean effect would not find it.

284 articles have zero citations. They are retained; a zero is informative about a paper nobody used and dropping them would condition on the outcome.

6. Exposure, and the gate

The exposure is the direction of the article’s own conclusion, and no rule extracts it from an abstract. This is the one study of the set whose exposure needs a model to read text, and that is a liability rather than a convenience.

Classification is therefore treated as an instrument that must be validated before it is used:

  1. A stratified random sample of 300 abstracts is classified by the author into inadequate, adequate, mixed, or not applicable. This is the reference standard. A model does not produce it, for the reason R01 already demonstrated: a model checking a rule another model wrote found a real defect and still left agreement with a person unknown.
  2. Two models from different providers classify the same 300, blind to the author’s labels and to each other.
  3. Agreement is reported as Cohen’s kappa against the reference standard and between models.
  4. If neither model reaches kappa 0.70 against the author’s labels, the study stops and the negative methodological result is published. Whether a language model can read the direction of a scientific conclusion is itself worth knowing.
  5. Only above the threshold does classification extend to the rest, with the measured misclassification rate carried into a sensitivity analysis.

The threshold is written here, before any classification has been run. A threshold chosen after seeing the agreement is not a threshold.

7. Analysis

In R.

glm.nb(cited_by_count ~ direction + poly(years_since_pub, 2) + topic_block + journal_size,
       data = d)

Age enters as a quadratic because citation accrual is not linear in time and the corpus spans 1965 to 2026. Preregistered alongside: quantile regression at the 75th and 90th percentiles, and the same model with direction as measured by each classifier separately, so that a result that depends on which model read the abstracts is visible as such.

8. Threats, and what happened to each

threat what was done removed or disclosed
Reverse causation Not addressable in this design. A widely cited article may attract a negative framing rather than the other way round Disclosed
Topic confounds direction Some topics almost never produce an adequate verdict, so adjusting for topic may absorb the effect. Reported adjusted and unadjusted, with the difference discussed rather than resolved Disclosed
Classifier error Gated on kappa against the author’s labels, with a stated stop Disclosed
Citation counts move Frozen at a dated snapshot in the repository Removed
Self-citation and field size Not in the primary model; OpenAlex exposes the data and it is a stated secondary Disclosed
Zero-citation articles Retained; dropping them conditions on the outcome Removed

11. What this cannot show

  • It cannot show that negative findings are rewarded, only that they are cited more.
  • It cannot separate the effect of the finding from the effect of the topic that produced it.
  • OpenAlex citation counts include self-citations and citations from any source it indexes, which is broader than PubMed and not identical to it.
