The agenda

Studies proposed against the map, published before they are run

Each proposal below is a protocol. It states a hypothesis, what would falsify it, the unit of analysis, the data source with a call that proved the source reachable, and the analysis in R, and it is published in that state before any analysis is run.

The order matters and it is deliberate. A protocol written after the result is known is not a protocol, and a project that criticizes selective reporting cannot itself decide what its question was once it has seen the answer. Every proposal therefore carries a date and a status, and a result page will link back to the protocol as it stood.

Status values

The distinction between a design and a protocol is not cosmetic. It comes from the sibling project’s design standard and it is the rule this project works to: the difference between a design and a protocol is a set of computed numbers. A document that asserts its sample size, its power or its feasibility rather than measuring them is a design, whatever it calls itself.

Every document from P10 onward is written against this project’s own design standard, twelve requirements each traced to the review finding that produced it.

status meaning
design the question, hypothesis, threats and probes are written. Feasibility is not yet measured
protocol-pending-validation probes computed and written in. One or more validation gates need human labels before it runs
protocol-reduced probes computed, and they came back small enough that a pre-written rule cut the analysis down
blocked-by-review two reviewers found an evidenced fatal flaw that stands unrebutted. Left as submitted
agenda a real gap that public data cannot answer, recorded rather than skipped
reported result published with the protocol and, when they exist, the reviews

Nothing here is registered. Registration needs the probes complete, the validation gates passed, and two reviews attached. No document has all three.

Review

Review is by two models from different providers, each given the document and instructed to refute it, with output validated against a schema and adjudicated by the fixed rule in build/audit/adjudicate.py. That rule downgrades an unsubstantiated severe finding rather than counting it, lets a lone reviewer holding a verbatim quote block a document two others called sound, and publishes a contested document as contested rather than picking a winner. Twelve golden cases pin it. No document is reviewed by one model alone.

Five documents have been reviewed and all five were blocked: P01v2, P02, P03, P05 and P06, each on evidenced fatal findings. The findings for P01v2 and P02 were checked against primary source and are published at the reviews page. The verdicts for P03, P05 and P06 are recorded in data/audit/ and have not yet been acted on or published as pages; that is pending.

P10 to P29 have not been reviewed. They were written after those five reviews, against a standard distilled from them, and their review is scheduled for later. The designs are reviewed when their probes have run, because a reviewer asked to assess feasibility numbers that do not exist yet is being asked to review a wish.

How these were chosen

The selection rule was to turn the field’s own instrument on the field. Meta-research measures whether others share data, cite retracted work, follow checklists, replicate, and report inconvenient findings. Those questions had not been asked about meta-research itself, and most are answerable from data already harvested or openly available.

The rule also kept out the obvious alternative. A study measuring transparency, or FAIRness, in one more specialty is a rate in a new population, and rates in new populations are what this corpus already holds in quantity. Every document here has an exposure, an outcome and a direction, and every one can come out the other way.

The second round, P10 to P29, kept that rule where it applied and widened it where the author’s own tools made a sharper question possible: rtransparency and rfair are validated instruments, and several designs exist because a validated instrument turns a vague question into a measurable one. Candidates were dropped when a relevance-ordered search found the question already answered; the table below lists them.

P04 and P30 are not runnable and say so. P09 is the most important question of the set and is the least far along, which is a fact about difficulty rather than about priority.
